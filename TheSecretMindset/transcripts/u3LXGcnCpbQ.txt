Three of those waves (1, 3 and 5) move in the direction of the underlying trend, while

Well, because Elliot waves are subjective, I decided to focus only on the first 3-waves: an impulsive wave, a corrective price move and another impulsive move, which is the price movement we will trade. The 3-wave pattern is used as a continuation trading setup that is designed to take advantage of the second impulsive move of the market. In any up trending market, there is a pattern of higher highs and higher lows.

In an uptrend market situation, price will make 3 points Point A is the lowest low point, which forms a support level.
[02:30] Point B will be the highest point, and forms a level that we consider as potential resistance Point C will be the 2nd low point, a support level (which must be higher than point A). The breakout of price above point B signals the continuation of the uptrend. In a downtrend market, the 3-wave pattern forms when: Point A becomes the highest point when price finds resistance and moves down. Point B becomes the lowest low point (and forms a level that we consider as potential
[03:00] support) and Then price moves up and finds another resistance at point C. When price breaks the point B support level, it indicates that the market is most likely to continue downward The most important variable in this strategy is the swing size, the first AB wave, because our risk to reward ratio will depend on the strength of the first impulse wave.

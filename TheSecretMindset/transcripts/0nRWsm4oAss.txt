The Alligator is trading indicator created by famous trader Bill Williams, which, just like moving averages, is used to confirm ongoing trends and their primary direction. In addition to identifying existing trends, you can also use the alligator indicator to enter counter trend moves. Alligator is a trend-following tool which follows the premise that financial markets
[00:30] and individual stocks trend just 15% to 30% of the time while grinding through sideways ranges the other 70% to 85% of the time. The indicator has a funny name: it comes from the head of an alligator. As part of it, there are three averages. Each average has a name related to an alligator’s mouth: jaws, teeth and lips. The alligator analogy is designed to help describe some of the behaviors of the market
[01:00] as it goes from non-trending to trending. The idea is that the absence of a trend in the market — meaning periods of sideways movement — is like a sleeping alligator. Eventually the alligator wakens and the longer it has slept, the hungrier will be — in other words, the more pronounced the trend will be. I know this analogy might seem funny, but don’t exit the video yet and let me explain further the usefulness of this indicator.
[01:30] Bill Williams was insistent that a successful trader will know the structure of the market. In keeping with this line of thinking, the Alligator indicator consists of three moving averages, each smoothed over different time-frames, which he called 'balance lines'. By looking at the behavior of the balance lines, we can determine the structure of the market — in other words, whether we are dealing with a sleeping alligator or not. So Alligator uses three smoothed moving averages, set at five, eight and 13 periods, which are
[02:00] all Fibonacci numbers. The three moving averages comprise the Jaw, Teeth, and Lips of the Alligator, opening and closing in reaction to evolving trends and trading ranges: Green moving average represents the lips of the Alligator and is set to 5 and displaced into the future by 3 periods Red moving average is the Alligator teeth
[02:30] and is set to 8 periods and is pushed 5 periods into the future Blue represents the jaws of the Alligator and is set to 13 periods and displaced 8 periods into the future Now, How to Use the Alligator Indicator in Trading There are three key pieces of information shown by the Alligator indicator: the absence of a trend, the formation of a trend, and the direction of the trend.
[03:00] Let's discuss each of these, and see how we can use them with Alligator indicator: First. The Absence of a Trend. This is a common state and it's indicated by those times when the three lines of the Alligator indicator are close together or intersected. We can see it in this example, where the green, the red, and the blue lines all stick close together. This means that the alligator is dormant — and that the market is not really doing anything,
[03:30] and that we are awaiting a change in state. So when the 3 lines are intersected, we do not have a trend. This is generally a time of low volatility and you may want to find another instrument to trade. Next. The Formation of a Trend A successful awakening of the alligator is a crossover of the fast green line through the slower lines, plus the slower lines following that direction, and all three lines spreading apart.
[04:00] When the moving averages move wide apart like in this example, the alligator is woke and eating. The longer the alligator has lain dormant, the hungrier it is. I know is cringey, but think of a market that is ranging… we know it will break out and the longer the range continues, the more violent the breakout can be. These periods of persistent trends are when the indicator tends to be more effective.
[04:30] So, the lips of the Alligator, the green line, the fastest moving average, will be the first one you will want to monitor. You want to see the green line cross both of the slower moving averages. You will also see the lips and the jaw start to turn in the direction of the green line. As an entry point, many traders will enter the market following a candle close above/below all 3 lines. Third phase. Direction of the Trend The direction is indicated by the movement
[05:00] of the balance lines. The green line is our fastest moving average, and we expect this to move first, followed by the red line, and then the blue line. The green line crossing above the slower lines represents a buy signal. Crossing below represents a sell signal. If all three move higher and widen, it confirms an uptrend. If the balance lines move downward and widen after a sell signal, it confirms a downtrend.
[05:30] As the trend comes to an end, the balance lines draw closer together. The fast green line crossing back over the slower lines is the signal to take your profit. The key strength of the indicator is the way that it helps you to stick with a persistent trend. One of the drawbacks is the difficulty in successfully reading the opening signals in a timely manner. During sleeping period, when the lines are closed and intersected, you will see many

The trouble is the timing — because if the indicator lags at the start of a down trend for example, and the fall was steep, you could potentially miss the move. The classical way to use The Alligator is to look for “the perfect order”. The three moving averages should NOT cross. When doing that, the perfect order forms. Moreover, this signals a strong trend.
[06:30] But this isn’t the only way to use it. Remember that this indicator shifts the moving averages further in time. This shifting allows for a greater flexibility. In a way, it resembles the Ichimoku Cloud. Traders familiar with cloud strategies know the cloud, or “kumo” is shifted on the right side of the chart! Therefore, future support and resistance levels appear at the present time. A useful Alligator trading approach deals with support and resistance levels.

Dynamic support and resistance move along with the price. Therefore, they offer more value. The three moving averages that make the Alligator indicator do exactly that. They follow the price. If the price rises, the three lines follow. The bigger the distance between the lines, the stronger the support and resistance area.

In this way, the area between the first line (the green one – lips) and the last one (the blue one – jaws) is dynamic support or resistance. For example, in a bullish trend, it shows support.

The Wyckoff Method consists of a series of principles and strategies which can be applied to all sorts of financial markets. I will break Wyckoff’s method into 4 parts: • The three laws of Wyckoff
[00:30] • The Composite Man • Wyckoff Price Cycle • Wyckoff Volume Spread Analysis The three laws of Wyckoff Wyckoff's method rests on three fundamental “laws”. 1. The law of supply and demand When demand is greater than supply, prices rise, and when supply is greater than demand, prices fall. The trader can study the balance between supply and demand by comparing price and volume bars over time, as a way to better visualize the relation between supply and demand.
[01:00] This often provides insights into the next market movements. 2. The Law of Cause and Effect The second law states that the differences between supply and demand are not random. Instead, they come after periods of preparation, as a result of specific events. In Wyckoff's terms, a period of accumulation (cause) eventually leads to an uptrend (effect). In contrast, a period of distribution (cause) eventually results in a downtrend (effect).
[01:30] 3. The Law of Effort vs. Result The third Wyckoff law states that the changes in an asset’s price are a result of an effort, which is represented by the trading volume. If the price action is in harmony with the volume, there is a good chance the trend will continue. But, if the volume and price diverge, the market trend is likely to stop or change direction. For instance, imagine that a market starts to consolidate with a very high volume after a long bearish trend.
[02:00] The high volume indicates a big effort, but the sideways movement suggests a small result. So, there is a lot of activity, but no more significant price drops. Such a situation could indicate that the downtrend may be over, and a reversal is near. The Composite Man Wyckoff created the idea of the Composite Man as an imaginary identity of the market. He proposed that investors and traders should study the stock market as if a single entity was controlling it. It always acts in his own best interest to ensure he can buy low and sell high.
[02:30] Think of composite man as an evil entity, like the bad guy in a movie, sitting behind the scenes controlling markets by pushing buttons. His goal is to move the markets to your disadvantage. The composite man is continually seeking to maximize his profit at “your” expense. The Composite Man’s behavior is the opposite of retail traders, but according to Wyckoff, the Composite Man uses a somewhat predictable strategy, from which traders can learn from.
[03:00] Here are some main ideas of composite man: • Composite man plans and executes his deception well in advance • Composite man is there to fool you to buying positions that he has already accumulated • Composite man will tempt you to sell at the worst possible time • Composite man leaves subtle evidence of his strategy for those who understand him Now, let’s use the Composite Man concept to illustrate a market cycle. 1. Accumulation The process of accumulation is the first stage of the Wyckoff price cycle.
[03:30] The Composite Man accumulates assets before most investors. The accumulation is done gradually to avoid the price from changing significantly. Bulls are gaining power slowly and as a result, they are poised to push prices higher. Although the Accumulation stage is related with the bulls gaining authority, the price action on the chart is flat. In other words, the process of accumulation is illustrated by a ranging price structure on the chart. Higher lows within the range is usually considered a signal that the price action is currently
[04:00] in an Accumulation phase. 2. Markup Phase (Uptrend) The Markup is the second stage of the Wyckoff trading cycle. When the Composite Man is holding enough power, and the selling force is depleted, he starts pushing the market up. The emerging trend attracts more investors, causing demand to increase. There may be multiple phases of accumulation during an uptrend. We may call them re-accumulation phases, where the bigger trend stops and consolidates for a while, before continuing its upward movement.
[04:30] As the market moves up, other traders are encouraged to buy. At this point, demand is higher than supply. 3. Distribution The Distribution process is the third stage of the Wyckoff price cycle. Here, the Composite Man starts distributing his holdings. He sells his profitable positions to those entering the market at a late stage. The distribution phase is marked by a sideways movement that absorbs demand until it gets exhausted. The price action on the chart at this stage is flat.
[05:00] One indication that the market is in a Distribution stage will be the sustained failure of price to create higher lows on the chart. The price action creates lower highs which is an indication that the market is currently experiencing a selloff. 4. Markdown Phase (Downtrend) The Markdown is the last stage of the Wyckoff price cycle. Soon after the distribution phase, the market starts reverting to the downside. After the Composite Man is done selling a good amount of his shares, he starts pushing the market down. The supply becomes much greater than demand, and the downtrend is established.
[05:30] When the bearish trend is finally over, a new accumulation phase begins.

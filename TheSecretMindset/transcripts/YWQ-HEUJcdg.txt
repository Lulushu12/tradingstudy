Supply zones are found when the market makes a large move down from either a single candle or small consolidation structure, also known as a base. Demand zones are found when the market makes a large move up, again, from a single candle or a base. Drawing the supply and demand zones is simple. For a supply zone, you want to mark the area from the OPEN of the LAST bullish candle before
[00:30] the drop which created the supply zone, up to the most recent high before the drop. For a demand zone, you mark the area from the OPEN of the LAST bearish candle before the drop which created the demand zone, down to the most recent low before the up move. If you want to know more about how to find and draw supply and demand zones make sure you watch this video first. Now, there are 4 types of supply and demand, defined by how the price approaches the supply
[01:00] and demand zone and how it leaves the zone again. I’ve grouped them into continuation structures and reversal structures. Continuation price structures include Drop-base-Drop and Rally-base-Rally patterns. Reversal price structures are Drop-base-Rally and Rally-base-Drop patterns. So let’s analyze each of those structures, one by one. Rally – Base – Rally The rally-base-rally is a type of demand zone which forms during an up-move, being a continuation
[01:30] pattern. The market structure is very simple. In this example, you can see we first have a rally, then a consolidation (or the base) and finally another rally, which creates the demand zone. A rally-base-rally will always form a demand zone; it can never create a supply zone. Drop – Base – Drop The drop-base-drop is the exact opposite of a rally-base-rally, with the only similarity being they both form during trending movements. A drop-base-drop will always form a supply zone, a zone which if the market returns to,
[02:00] should push the price back in the direction of the movement that created the zone. In this example a drop lower created the supply zone so if the market will return to this zone it should push the market lower. Now the big question: what creates drop – base – drop/rally – base – rally structures? Both zones form in the market because of bank traders taking profits off existing trading positions. In this example, during the first rally we had bank traders placing long trades.
[02:30] When the bank traders are in profitable trades, they’ll want to secure some of their profits. When they decide to do this, they consume all of the buy orders coming into the market from retail traders who are have begun entering long trades due to size of the rally. This consumption of buy orders means the market makes a small move lower, and creates the base of the potential demand zone. The move lower causes a large number of retail traders who went long on the rally higher
[03:00] to close their trades at a loss which puts a lot of sell orders into the market. In addition to this, there will be a small number of retail traders thinking the move down is a trend reversal, meaning they’ll place sell trades with the expectation that the market is going to move lower. With these two sets of retail traders putting sell order in the market, the banks now begin buying again knowing they’ll make the market move higher, and in the process cause anybody who sold the move to close their trades at a loss, which ends up being the bank traders
[03:30] profit. For the drop-base-drop structure, the exact same process takes place only the other way around. The banks take profits from sell positions, which stops the market from falling, then as the market begins to retrace against the trend, the retail traders who sold late into the down-move close their trades at a loss, and reversal traders begin placing buy trades because they think the trend is changing. Both of these traders are putting buy orders into the market, the banks start selling again
[04:00] at the supply zone, and all the traders who placed buy trades are now losing money.
[05:00] Drop – Base – Rally The drop -base-rally is a structure which always forms a demand zone in the market. These zones differ from the first 2 price structures, mainly because of their location in the market. If rally-base-rally and drop-base-drop zones only form during trending movements, drop-base-rally zones will only be found when the market changes from moving down to moving up.
[05:30] So, it’s a reversal pattern. Rally – Base – Drop On the opposite side of the scale we have rally-base-drop zones. These zones form when the market reverses from moving higher to moving lower, and when their construction is complete, a supply zone will have formed in the market which should cause the market to fall. Again, a reversal structure. What Creates Rally – Base – Drop/Drop – Base – Rally structure? The drop-base-rally and rally-base-drop zones are created by bank traders either placing
[06:00] trades to make the market reverse or by taking profits off existing trading positions. Very important: The rally-base-drop/ drop-base-rally zones which form when bank traders taking profits have a lower probability of resulting in successful trades than zones that are created because of bank traders placing trades, wanting the market to reverse. To understand why, we must analyze and try to find the intentions of the bank traders when they are placing trades.
[06:30] If a bank is placing trades in order to make other traders lose money, the area at which they have placed their trades is important, as they have an interest in keeping the market price from breaking the area. In supply and demand trading this means when a rally-base-drop or drop-base-rally zone is created by bank traders placing trades, wanting a reverse the market, the banks must keep the market from moving past the point where they have entered their positions, in order to protect the trades they have placed.

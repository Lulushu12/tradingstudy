Elliott Wave Theory Elliott Wave Theory is all about identifying repeating movements  on price charts, which are called waves. These waves can be either impulsive  (moving with the trend) or corrective (moving against the trend). Impulsive waves represent the direction of the current trend. Corrective waves work against the prevailing trend and are usually weaker and less aggressive. An uptrend typically has three large upward
[13:30] price impulses and two corrections,  creating a five-wave pattern. This uptrend is then followed by three waves  lower: an impulse down, a correction to the upside, and another impulse down. There are three golden rules in Elliott Wave Theory: Wave 2 cannot go past the start of wave 1. Wave 3 cannot be the shortest wave (so wave  1 or wave 5, or both, must be shorter).
[14:00] And Wave 4 cannot go into the  price territory of wave 2 (so waves 2 and 4 must have clear price separation). It's best to trade in the direction of the impulse waves, as they represent the largest price moves. So in an uptrend, buy during pullbacks or at the end of a corrective wave, and  ride the next impulse wave higher. In a downtrend, sell at the end of a corrective  wave to profit from the next impulse wave down.

Gaps happen because of an imbalance between supply and demand. An up gap forms when a candle’s low price is higher than the previous candle’s high price. A down gap is the opposite – the high price is lower than the previous low price. Gaps show that something significant
[15:00] has changed in the fundamentals or the  psychology of the market participants. A gap fill is when the price  action retraces to the level before the gap. Breakaway gaps occur when the price breaks  out of a trading range or congestion area.
[15:30] They need market enthusiasm and a significant  increase in volume to happen. If the volume increases when the gap occurs, it means the  new trend has a good chance of continuing. Runaway gaps happen in the middle of a strong  trend and show that the trend is accelerating. They're a sign of increased enthusiasm  and usually happen with high volume.

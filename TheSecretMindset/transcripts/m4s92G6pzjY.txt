Use the right indicators to spot divergences

From my experience, oscillators that are used to determine whether a market is overbought or oversold are more effective for spotting hidden divergences. Stochastic indicator and RSI are perfect to find hidden divergences, which happens when the technical indicator shows a new extreme, but the corresponding price action does not.

In essence, while the price is higher than it was before, the indicator is lower, suggesting the market is much more oversold. In a hidden bearish divergence scenario, the Stochastic makes a higher high while the price makes a lower high. Hidden divergences are also called continuation divergences because are used to trade the continuation of a trend.
[01:30] For regular bullish and bearish indicators, I found that MACD histogram and Money flow index are very effective to find such patterns. Bullish divergences are used to trade the change in direction from a downwards move to an upwards move. They occur when price creates a lower low and at the same time, the technical indicator, MACD in this example, is making a higher low. In essence, MACD is not following the price down, suggesting the move lower is weakening and losing momentum, resulting in a possible move higher.

In this case, MACD is not following the price up, suggesting the move higher is weakening and losing momentum, resulting in a possible move lower. 2. Only take divergences in the direction of the main trend With regular divergences, the oscillators indicate momentum shift and thus, point toward a reversal of the trend.
[03:30] This implies that a regular divergence warns the end of a trend is expected. Hidden divergences signal momentum coming into the main trend, suggesting a possible continuation in the main direction of the trend. Now, the reason why so many traders fail when taking divergence signals is because they take counter-trend signals. They spot a regular divergence and immediately think the market will reverse. A smarter way to trade divergences, is to identify the main trend and find divergences that go along with the trend.
[04:00] In the case of an uptrend, you ignore bearish regular divergences (those appearing on the top of the oscillator) and search for bullish regular divergences. In addition, you also concentrate on bullish hidden divergences. In the case of a downtrend, you ignore bullish regular divergences and search for bearish regular divergences and bearish hidden divergences. In this way, you have a higher probability of a successful divergence trade. 4.

The best confirmation is a trend line break out. When a divergence forms and the price starts changing direction and breaks a trend line,

To correctly read the volume data, it must be seen in the light of what the price is doing.

[00:00] You’ve probably heard the word “pullback” or “retracement” or “correction” quite frequently if you’re interested in trading the financial markets. So, what is a pullback? A pullback in a market is a pretty easy concept to define and understand. It’s a period when price retraces back on a recent move, either up or down. It’s basically a reversal of a recent price move. Why are pullbacks important? For a number of reasons: they are opportunities to enter the market at a “better price”,
[00:30] they allow for optimal stop loss placement, improved risk reward and more. A pullback entry is more conservative than a “market entry” for example and is considered a “safer” entry type. Ultimately, the goal of a trader is obtain the best entry price and manage risk as good as possible whilst also increasing returns and the pullback entry is a tool that allows you to do all three of these things. So here is why pullback trading is very important: First, when identified correctly, will offer
[01:00] you higher probability entries The very nature of a pullback means that price is likely to continue moving in the direction of the initial move when the retrace ends. Hence, if you see a strong price action signal at a level following a retracement, it’s a high-probability entry because all signs are pointing to price bouncing from that point. Now, it doesn’t always happen, but waiting for a retrace to a level with a signal, is
[01:30] the highest-probability way you can trade. Markets rotate back to the “mean” or “average” price over and over; this is clear by looking at any price chart. So, when you see this retrace happen, start looking for an entry point there because it’s a much higher-probability entry point than simply entering “at market” like most traders do. Second, it offers better risk rewards.
[02:00] Pullback entries theoretically allow you to place a “smaller” stop losses on a trade because you’re entering closer to a key level. So, if you choose to do so, you can place a stop much closer. For example: a 100 pips stop and 200 pips target can easily become a 50 pip stop and 250 pip target on a retrace entry. Of course: you don’t need to place a smaller stop loss, it’s optional, but the option
[02:30] is there on a pullback entry if you want it. And, at the same time, a pullback entry allows more flexibility with your stop loss placement. Of course, there are some negative aspects in what concerns waiting for a pullback. You will have less trades in general. A lot of the time, markets simply don’t retrace enough to trigger the more conservative entry that comes with a pull back. Instead, they may just keep going with minimal retracements. This means you will have less chances to trade as compared to someone who isn’t primarily
[03:00] waiting for pullbacks. Also, you will have more missed trades. Some potential good trades will “get away” when waiting for a retracement that doesn’t happen, for example. This can test your nerves and will annoy you sometimes. But, missing out on trades is not the worst thing in the world and it’s better to miss out on some trades than to over-trade.

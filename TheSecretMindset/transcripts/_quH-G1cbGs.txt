Renko charts are a new way to view the price of an asset that filters out minor price movements.
[00:30] The chart looks like a series of bricks placed at a 45-degree angle to each other. If you analyze these 2 two charts, you immediately spot the striking differences between a regular candlestick chart and a Renko chart. You can see that those small fluctuations on classic charts are removed, and price trends are easier to spot on the Renko chart. Renko charts are easier to read because the markers on the chart—which are called bricks, boxes, or blocks—are more uniform compared to candlestick charts.
[01:00] Traditional candlestick charts plot new bars as time passes. For instance, a one-minute chart will plot a new bar every minute. On the other hand, a Renko chart: • Does not depend on time • Plots a new bar only when the market moves beyond a certain amount Being independent of time is the defining characteristic of a Renko chart. So, it depends only on price action, creating a smoothed chart with less noise. Each brick on a Renko chart is a candlestick with no shadows.
[01:30] You need only one key parameter for Renko charts – the brick size. The brick size is the price range that the market must cover before a new brick is plotted. You can set the brick size in pips, ticks, or cents, depending on the market you’re trading. And, for a Renko chart to reverse, meaning the brick to change its color, price action must reverse by at least two brick sizes. Let’s say you trade Forex, and we’ll pick the EUR/JPY pair. It you want to set your brick size to 5 pips for example, you just have to adjust the settings
[02:00] of the Renko box size to 0.05. If you want a ten pip box size, you set it 0.1. I use Tradingview, by the way, to trade with Renko bars. For GBP/USD or EUR/USD, for a 10 pip brick size you set the box size to 0.001. You can set the values of the box size depending on your trading style: scalping, day trading or swing trading, and you can change the value of the Renko chart to suit your trading needs.
[02:30] If you trade stocks, a box size of 1, is the equivalent of 1 dollar. I find this value to be perfect for swing trading and even day trading strategies. Every time the stock moves 1 dollar, a new brick is formed. Since the brick size is the only parameter, a successful Renko strategy is highly dependent on it. There are 2 methods used to set this parameter. Fixed Amount Average True Range First, let’s talk about the FIXED AMOUNT method.
[03:00] Using a set number of ticks/pips/cents as the brick size is the simplest method. But unless you are already familiar with the market, it’s challenging to arrive at a suitable figure. You need some trial and error here. If you want to see more bricks plotted, reduce the size the box size. This approach is indicated if you prefer scalping or day trading. If you enjoy swing trading, increase the box size, and in this way less bricks will be plotted.
[03:30] Be careful not to use a brick size that’s too small. If your brick size is too small, the Renko chart will be volatile. As a result, it loses its intended effect of smoothing price action. There is no rule that works for every currency pair or stock. When I use Renko, I just eyeball the chart, and try a few different box sizes. If it is too choppy I try increasing the box size. Then I try decreasing it, because maybe there are trends to be captured within the choppy trading.
[04:00] Smaller boxes mean more trend changes and more trades. Big boxes mean fewer trend changes and fewer trades. Second approach involves the AVERAGE TRUE RANGE (ATR) for brick sizes The advantage of this method is that it factors in changes in price volatility. A typical value is the 14-period ATR. So the brick size is based on the ATR value at the time the chart is created. Should the ATR value change the next day, then this new ATR value will be used to set
[04:30] the brick size. Which one is better? I prefer the fixed value because the ATR value might change and can cause the Renko chart to change significantly whenever it is updated. That’s just my preference, but I know a few traders that use the ATR Brick size successfully. One characteristic of Renko charts that should be understood is that the charts don’t show all price points. Consider that you are using a Renko brick of 3 pips and price moves 5 pips in the opposite direction.
[05:00] Since that is not enough price movement to make another Renko brick, there will be 2 pips of price that does not show on the Renko chart. When I first started day trading Renko day charts, this bothered me a lot, especially after trading with time and classic candlesticks for so many years. There are indicators that add ‘wicks’ to the Renko bricks to show these prices, but these start to add some of the ‘noise’ back to the chart. It’s up to you if you want to add or remove the wicks on Renko bars.
[05:30] So the most important step in creating a successful Renko strategy is setting the size of the brick. It may be $0.10 in the stock market or 10 pips in forex market. What you must remember is that a brick forms on the Renko chart once the price has moved that amount, and not before. A candlestick chart, on the other hand, shows the price movement over a period of time, such as one minute or one day. While there is a time axis along the bottom of a Renko chart, there is no set time limit for how long a Renko box takes to form.
[06:00] It could take 4 minutes, three hours, or eight days. It all depends on how volatile the pricing of the asset is and what brick size you set. So Renko charts ignore the time aspect and only focus on price changes. If the brick value is set at 10 points, a move of 10 points or more is required to draw another brick. Price movements less than 10 points would be ignored and the Renko chart would remain unchanged. Renko charts are most useful to day traders and scalpers for spotting trends, areas of

Thus, it’s easy to spot important highs and lows, and identify key support and resistance levels.

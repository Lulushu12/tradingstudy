[00:00] The Ichimoku Cloud is a versatile indicator that defines support and resistance, identifies trend direction, shows momentum and provides trading signals. Even though the Ichimoku Cloud may seem complicated when viewed on the price chart, it's actually a rather straightforward indicator; the concepts are easy to understand and the signals are well-defined. Ichimoku takes multiple averages, plots them on the chart and uses these figures to compute a "cloud" which attempts to forecast where the price may find support or resistance in
[00:30] the future. The Ichimoku Cloud consists of five lines. Conversion Line, Base Line, Lagging Span, Leading Span A and Leading Span B. First component: Conversion Line, also known as Tenkan-Sen The Conversion Line shows the short-term price momentum. On its own, it shows the mid-point price over the last 9 periods, which is the default value for this indicator.
[01:00] Conversion Line signals an area of minor support or resistance. • If the market price is above the conversion line, this indicates a short-term upward momentum, and you would want to concentrate on buying signals • If the price is below conversion line, this shows a short-term downward momentum, and you would want to search for selling signals • An increasing conversion line, meaning an upward slope, indicates an upward trend • A decreasing conversion line, meaning
[01:30] a downward slope, indicates a downward trend Due to the very short-term nature of the indicator, it is not typically used on its own but rather used in conjunction with the other elements of the Ichimoku Cloud. Second component is the base line, also known as the Kijun-sen or the confirmation line. The base line serves as a signal for support and resistance levels on medium term. The base Line is the mid-point of the high and low price over the last 26 periods.
[02:00] On its own, the base line can also be used for analyzing price momentum. • When the price is above the base line, it means the price is above 26-period mid-point and therefore has an upward bias. • If the price is below the base Line, it is below the mid-point price, and therefore has a downward bias. • An increasing slope of the base line indicates an upward trend • A decreasing slope of the base line indicates a downward trend
[02:30] Unless there is a very strong trend, the base line will often appear near the price. When the base Line is often intersecting or near the price, it is not as useful for helping to evaluate the trend direction. Third component: lagging span, also referred to as Chikou Span The lagging span is created by plotting closing prices 26 periods behind the latest closing price of an instrument. This lagging line is designed to allow traders to visualize the relationship between current
[03:00] and prior trends, as well as to spot potential trend reversals. One of the key ways to use to the indicator is to view its relationship to the current price. • When the price is below the lagging span, this is usually an indication there is strength in the price and it is moving higher. • When the price appears above the lagging line, this is often an indication there is weakness in the price. If it appears that the lagging line is about to cross above the prior price line, it would
[03:30] be a bullish confirmation sign, while a span that is crossing underneath would be a bearish confirmation sign. Trading signals based on the Chikou span are strongest when it doesn’t touch or cross over any the prior candles. Any interaction with the past price line is an indication of a choppy or sideways market. If a lagging line is descending quickly into a past price line, it could be a sign of exhaustion
[04:00] for the asset. Forth component: Kumo cloud, which is formed from 2 lines (leading span a and leading span b) The Kumo Cloud provides support/resistance levels that can be projected into the future. This sets the Ichimoku Cloud apart from many other technical indicators that only provide support and resistance levels for the current date and time. The uptrend is strengthened when the Leading Span A (green cloud line) is rising and above
[04:30] the Leading Span B (red cloud line). This situation produces a green cloud. Conversely, a downtrend is reinforced when the Leading Span A (green cloud line) is falling and below the Leading Span B (red cloud line). This situation produces a red cloud. Because the cloud is shifted forward 26 periods, it also provides a glimpse of future support or resistance. If it is thick, then the support/resistance is strong (depending on the position of price
[05:00] in relation to the cloud). By contrast, if it is thin, then the S/R levels are considered weak. Generally, if the price is above the Kumo, then there is an uptrend in place and/or more buying opportunities. If the price is below the Kumo, then it is under resistance and it is better to be looking for shorts instead of longs. The longer price action stays above or below the Kumo, the stronger the trend and the more
[05:30] support/resistance the Kumo offers. The Kumo can be very effective for long term traders as well as for trend/momentum traders. This is because price will stay on one side of the Kumo cloud during a trend. The farther the price action is from it, the stronger the trend and more volatile it is. Generally, you should avoid trading inside the Kumo cloud, as it signals a period of indecision. As a type of moving average system, Ichimoku signals are generated by crossovers of the
[06:00] key lines, namely conversion line and base line. When the Tenkan/conversion line crosses over the Kijun/base line, it generates a buying signal. A signal to sell appears when the Tenkan/conversion line crosses below the Kijun/ base line. But in Ichimoku theory, the signals are not created equal, and this is where the cloud comes into play. If the two lines cross to the downside while current prices are above the cloud, only a
[06:30] weak sell signal is generated. The strongest sell signal appears when the downside crossover occurs while prices are below the cloud, essentially signaling that fresh weakness is now evident within an overall downtrend. The same guidelines apply to upside crossovers generating buy signals and the location of current prices to the cloud, with the strongest buy signal coming from an upside crossover
[07:00] while prices are above the cloud. Here are several examples of conversion line - base line crossovers, with Kumo cloud as filter. The chikou/lagging span provides yet another way to evaluate the strength of the crossover
[07:30] signals given by the tenkan/conversion line and kijun/base lines. Remember that the chikou/lagging span is simply today’s closing price projected 26 days into the past, providing a clear look at today’s market level relative to earlier prices. If a sell signal is generated on a downside crossover and the chikou/lagging span is below the price candles, the signal is considered stronger. If a crossover to the upside occurs and the chikou/lagging span is above prices, then
[08:00] a higher probability is attached to the buy signal. Again, the idea is that a crossover in the direction of the overall trend is considered more powerful than one that is against the trend. The crossover between span lines forming the Kumo cloud is another trading strategy used by traders, in a similar fashion to a moving average crossover. However, remember that the lines of the cloud are projected forward by 26 periods.
[08:30] • when leading span A of the Kumo cloud crosses above span b, and prices are positioned above the Kumo cloud, we have a strong buy signal • when leading span A of the Kumo cloud crosses below span b, and prices are positioned below the Kumo cloud, we have a strong sell signal • when leading span A of the Kumo cloud crosses above span b, but prices are positioned below the Kumo cloud, we have a weak buy signal • when leading span A of the Kumo cloud
[09:00] crosses below span b, but prices are positioned above the Kumo cloud, we have a weak sell signal Another trading technique involves the Kumo cloud breakout. This signal occurs when the price cuts through the Kumo cloud. • when the price enters the Kumo cloud and breaks its upper line, we have a bullish signal • when the price enters the Kumo and breaks its lower line, we have a bearish signal If the price is clearly above or below it, then when price action breaks the kumo, it
[09:30] can often signal a reversal. There are several factors which increase the chances of a reversal, such as: · The thickness of the Kumo when broken · The amount of time price action has been on one side of it · How far price action has moved before touching/breaking the Kumo · and also the time frame Ichimoku relies on only three different time periods in its calculations: nine, 26 and 52. Japanese financial markets used to be open for trading on Saturdays, meaning that the
[10:00] trading week was six days long. As a result, the number nine represents a week and a half of trading; 26 equals the number of trading days in a typical month (30 minus four Sundays); and 52 equals two months of trading days. Japanese markets now trade only five days per week, so some practitioners of Ichimoku suggest revising the parameters to seven, 22 and 44.
[10:30] In my experience, however, the traditional default settings of nine, 26 and 52 have performed the best. Ichimoku does have its limitations. Let’s face it, the indicator can make a chart look busy with all the lines. To remedy this, depending on your trading needs, certain lines can be hidden. For example, all the lines can be hidden except for the Leading Span A and B which create the cloud. You need to focus on which lines provide the most information for you, and then consider
[11:00] hiding the rest if all the lines are distracting. Another limitation of the Ichimoku Cloud is that it is based on historical data. While two of these data points are plotted in the future, there is nothing in the formula that is predictive. Averages are simply being plotted in the future. The cloud can also become irrelevant for long periods of time, as the price remains way above or below it. At times like these, the conversion line, base line, and their crossovers become more

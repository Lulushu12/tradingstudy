The video critiques support/resistance mechanics (stops hunted at common levels), excessive confirmations (which compress risk/reward), RSI/momentum divergence (momentum weakness ≠ reversal signal), head-and-shoulders/double-bottom patterns (effectiveness based on stop-loss clustering, not pattern shape), and Fibonacci (self-fulfilling prophecy). ## Notable trading concepts and critiques

**Support and Resistance Trap** [00:00-01:00]:
- Common strategy: "Buy on the fifth 'textbook' touch of the support level"
- Why it fails: Each touch consumes buy orders; number of orders decreases with each touch
- By fourth touch: "Institutional investors...establish short positions near your long entry point"
- "You think you're buying into a strong stock, but in reality, you're buying into their exit strategy"
- Rule: "Did it touch a certain price level more than three times? I will not buy"
- Alternative: "Observe it break out, and then reverse my strategy"

**Stop-Loss Placement Error** [01:00-02:00]:
- Problem: Placing stops at entry point (no structural significance)
- "Normal trends will have pullbacks—this is an inevitable result of a healthy trend"
- "When price retraces to your entry point (which is structurally meaningless), your stop-loss order will be triggered"
- "Then, the price will continue to move in the direction you expected"
- New rule: "Track stop-loss orders to a price level that has structural significance. A price level that the market cares about, not just the price level you care about"

**First Pullback After Trend Reversal** [02:00-02:30]:
- "The first pullback after a true trend reversal is particularly significant"
- "Because the trend is new"
- "Traders trapped by old trend are still forced to exit"
- "What you're buying isn't weakness, but the pressure from all those who misjudged the situation"
- Risk/reward degradation: "By the third pullback, you've missed the best opportunity"
- Strategy: "First pullback is the time to add to your position. After that, reduce your position every time there is a pullback"

**Multiple Confirmations Trap** [02:30-03:30]:
- Example: Wait for "three signals appear simultaneously: indicator and pattern consistent, candlestick pattern consistent, intuition consistent"
- Risk/reward compression example:
  - Enter on price rebound: 4R (4 times risk)
  - After confirmation: 2.5R
  - After three confirmations: Only 1R
- "Every confirmation you wait for means the price is moving away from your stop-loss level and towards your target level"
- "You are not reducing risk, but rather compressing your returns while keeping the direction unchanged"
- Rule: "If the price is at the correct level and a rebound occurs, that is a confirmation signal. Everything that followed was just fear plus extra steps"

**VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)** [03:30-04:00]:
- "It is a line. It's like a moving average in a suit"
- "The benchmark for institutional funds is the volume-weighted average price"
- "Their execution algorithm revolves around VWAP transactions"
- "VWAP is the most reliable reference level on your screen"
- Specific setups: "First rebound after hitting bottom. The second test allowed them to gain a firm foothold"

**RSI/Momentum Divergence** [04:00-05:00]:
- Definition: "Prices hit new highs, but indicators are hitting even lower new highs; a reversal is imminent"
- Speaker's experience: "I held a short position for three weeks because the divergence was very obvious. Technically correct. But I've been losing money"
- What divergence actually means: "Momentum is weakening"
- Analogy: "A car traveling at 100 miles per hour slows down to 80 miles per hour; its speed is still 80 miles per hour. It will still knock you down"
- Rule: "Treat it as background information, not a trigger signal"

**Chart Patterns (Head-and-Shoulders, Double Bottom)** [05:00-06:00]:
- Common misconception: Patterns work because of "loss of market confidence" (head-and-shoulders) or "buyers entering twice" (double bottom)
- Reality: "The pattern is effective because of the location of the stop-loss level"
- Mechanism: "Below the neckline, the stop-loss levels for all traders who bought the right shoulder are clustered together"
- "Institutions will push prices through these stop-loss levels to complete their entry"
- "Patterns are simply a visual map of the location where liquidity accumulates. When liquidity is insufficient, the pattern becomes invalid"
- Rule: "Before trading any pattern, find the location of your stop-loss order, not the shape of the pattern"

**Delta Divergence (Order Flow)** [06:00-07:30]:
- Definition: "Measures real-time trades made by actual traders with real money"
- Question: "Are people more actively buying or selling now?"
- Signal: "When prices keep climbing to new highs, but there are fewer and fewer active buyers at the selling price level"
- Example: "Price has risen three times in a row, and each time Delta has decreased. This is a clear reversal signal"
- Certainty level: "Does Delta decrease when prices hit a new high? I'm ready for a reversal. There are no exceptions"
- Caveat: "This is not an indicator, but rather a reflection of order flow"

**Liquidity Sweep Interpretation** [07:00-07:30]:
- Problem: Mistaking which orders are being swept
- "The sweeping notification only tells you that the order has been completed"
- Direction determination: "A trend chart on a higher time frame can tell you the direction, not a sweeping pattern"
- Rule: "Never just look at a sweeping pattern"

**Trade with the Trend (Higher Timeframe)** [07:30-08:30]:
- "When the weekly chart shows an upward trend, institutional funds will also flow in this direction"
- "Trading against the trend in a lower timeframe might seem like a quick, short-term trade, but statistically speaking, it's tantamount to going against the current"
- Outcome: "With the same settings, but two different trend backgrounds, the results are completely different"
- "A longer time frame is the trend. You can't predict it, nor can you fight it; you can only go with the flow"

**Fibonacci Levels** [08:30-09:30]:
- Classification: "Self-fulfilling prophecy, not a mathematical principle"
- Why sometimes work: "Enough traders are paying attention to them that orders temporarily accumulate there"
- Hindsight bias: "If you look at enough charts, you can always find Fibonacci levels near reversal points"
- Qualified use: "When the Fibonacci horizontal line coincides with an existing structural horizontal line, this intersection does indeed create a denser cluster of orders"
- Rule: "Is there a structural reason why the Fibonacci horizontal line appears there? No? Ignore it"

**Support/Resistance vs. Supply/Demand Zones** [10:00-10:30]:
- Observation: "Open any chart and draw support/resistance levels. Now draw supply/demand zones. It's always the same area"
- Reality: "The terminology has changed, the scope has broadened, and the explanations sound more institutional. However, their operating mechanisms are exactly the same"
- "You've marked the point where the price previously reversed, and you expect a reaction when it reverses again"
- "The name itself does not determine its effectiveness; what truly matters is the quality of the horizontal line"

**Entry Timing vs. Candle Close** [10:30-11:00]:
- Comparison example:
  - Enter on price rebound: 10-point stop, 50-point target = 5R
  - Enter at candle close: 22-point stop, 38-point target = 1.7R
  - Same underlying setup, different entries, 3.3R difference in return
- "The same transaction. You lost 3.3R for a completely illusory sense of security"
- Rule: "A price rebound is the real signal. When the candlestick chart closes, you're simply confirming a fact you already knew—only at a higher cost"

**Point of Control (POC) / Volume Control Points** [11:00-12:30]:
- Definition: "Points with the highest trading volume within a specific period"
- Institutional use: "Original intention is to conduct transactions at prices close to fair value"
- Mechanism: "Only in this way can large orders be completed without driving up prices"
- Observation: "Each exposed POC on the chart acts like a magnet. When the price approaches it, I will observe the reaction and take action"
- Example: "Yesterday's POC: A clean and decisive response during today's trading session"

**Multi-Timeframe Analysis** [12:30-13:00]:
- Example: Pin bar on daily chart looks like bullish reversal, but entry/stop placement unclear
- Solution: "Zoom in to 5-minute chart. The low-point sweep, absorption, and clean reversal sequence accurately tell you where to enter and set strict stop loss"
- Framework: "The daily chart hides all useful information within a single candlestick. What looks like noise on a lower timeframe is actually a trading opportunity"
- Principle: "Institutional transactions occur on a lower timeframe. A higher timeframe gives you direction. A lower timeframe gives you precision. You need both"

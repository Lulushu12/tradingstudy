## Summary

This video teaches two related price action trading strategies based on smart money concepts: Break of Structure (BOS), Change of Character (CHOCH), and supply/demand flips. The first strategy trades pullbacks to previously-rejected supply or demand levels after structural breaks. The second strategy, the Rejection Block method, identifies areas of rejected price movement (marked by wicks and high volume) and enters on retests of those levels. Both strategies rely on price action, volume confirmation, and market structure without indicator settings.

## Strategy 2: Rejection Block Strategy

### Indicators and settings

- Volume: high volume confirmation required, but threshold NOT STATED
- Wicks: minimum "2 wicks rejection" [14:00], defined as "at least two consecutive candles with long wicks extending in the same direction" [14:00]

### Context / bias filter

"This strategy is mainly based on simple market structure movements. It assumes that large institutional traders move the market in ways that often trap retail traders" [02:30]. "Often, these rejection blocks form during bounces in a larger downtrend. By entering on the retest, you're trying to catch the next wave down in the trend" [21:00-21:30].

### Entry trigger (Short Signal - Rejection of Higher Prices)

[14:00-20:00] "The first key element of this method is spotting a '2 wicks rejection.' If there are more wicks, even better. This occurs when we see at least two consecutive candles with long wicks extending in the same direction" [14:00]. "In the context of a downtrend, we're looking for minimum two candles with long upper wicks" [14:30]. "The next crucial element is the presence of high volume on the candles containing these rejection wicks" [15:00]. "Once we've identified the 2 wicks rejection and confirmed high volume, we can define the 'rejection block'. This is the price range encompassing the wicks of these rejection candles" [16:00]. "After the initial rejection and the subsequent move lower, we often see price return to retest the rejection block" [16:30]. "When price comes back to test the rejection block, we pay close attention. We're looking for signs that sellers are still interested. This might look like another rejection wick, a series of small bearish candles, or a sharp drop off the level" [20:00]. "If we have this confirmation, we short the market when price retests the rejection block and shows signs of respecting it" [20:00].

### Entry trigger (Long Signal - Rejection of Lower Prices)

[23:00-25:00] "The first step is to spot two or more candles with long lower wicks. Sellers tried to push the price down, but buyers stepped in and pushed it back up" [23:00]. "Also, we want to see high volume on these rejection candles" [23:00]. "After spotting the rejection wicks and confirming high volume, we draw the rejection block. This area will act as a key support zone where buyers have shown they're ready to jump in" [23:30]. "We wait for the price to move away and then come back to test this level again" [23:30]. "While we wait, we're watching to see how the market acts near this level. Does it start to slow down as it approaches? Do we see little hesitation candles?" [24:00]. "Basically, we're looking for signs that buyers are still interested. Maybe another rejection wick, a series of small bullish candles, or even a sharp bounce off the level. If you see signs of rejection, you enter a buy trade" [24:00-24:30].

### Stop loss

"You place your stop loss above the rejection block. This level has already shown strong selling pressure, so a move above it might indicate that the setup has failed" [18:00] (for short signals). For long signals: "placing your stop loss just below the rejection block" [25:00].

### Take profit / exit

"For take profit, you can use previous areas or key technical levels below the current price" [18:00] (for short signals). NOT STATED for long signals.

### Invalidation / skip conditions

"Sometimes, the market might briefly dip below your identified rejection block before bouncing back up. But this doesn't necessarily invalidate your trade setup. Market Noise is a major factor for this. Short-term price movements can be influenced by big orders or algorithmic trading. These can cause brief dips below your rejection block" [25:30-26:00]. "A short break below a rejection block could simply be a 'shake-out of weak hands'" [26:00]. "To deal with these situations, instead of entering as soon as price touches the rejection block, wait for a candle to close above it" [26:30]. "A true breakout of the rejection block often comes with high volume. If you see price moving below your rejection block but volume is low, it might be a false move" [26:30].

## Summary
This video teaches Andrews Pitchfork (also called "median lines"), a versatile price action tool consisting of three parallel lines: a median line and two outer lines representing dynamic support and resistance. The video explains how to construct the pitchfork by selecting three pivot points, emphasizes proper interpretation of the median line (as an attractor rather than support/resistance), and presents multiple trading strategies including bounce trading, breakout trading, and combinations with RSI divergence detection.

## Strategy 1: Andrews Pitchfork Support/Resistance Channel Trading

### Indicators and settings
Andrews Pitchfork construction (3 pivot points):
- Point A: Beginning of trend [01:00]
- Point B: Reaction high after beginning [01:00]
- Point C: Reaction low after beginning [01:00]
Resulting lines:
- ML (Median Line): Center line, acts as "magnet" for price [02:30]
- UML (Upper Median Line): Support/resistance level [05:00]
- LML (Lower Median Line): Support/resistance level [05:00]
- Schiff line: Connects points A and C, used to determine trend end [06:00]

### Context / bias filter
Must identify correct trend beginning (point A) and reaction high/low (points B, C) [01:30]
"The selection of these points is somewhat subjective, but it's a good idea to experiment with different reaction highs and lows to find what works best." [01:00]

### Entry trigger
Price approaches support/resistance levels (UML and LML)
"Traders often look for buying or selling opportunities as the price approaches the ends of these channels." [00:00]

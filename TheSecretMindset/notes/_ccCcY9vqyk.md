### Supply vs. Demand: Definition

**Supply Zone** [00:30-01:00]:
- A price area **above** the current price where there is strong selling interest
- Where many sellers are located
- When price reaches this level, sell orders get filled and price moves down
- Results in selling pressure and price rejection

**Demand Zone** [01:00-01:30]:
- A price area **below** the current price where there is strong buying interest
- Where many buyers are located
- When price reaches this level, buying pressure causes prices to bounce up

**Core Concept** [00:00-01:30]: "A supply or demand imbalance around that price... Supply and demand zones are advanced concepts of support and resistance. These zones are the areas that show high liquidity and pending orders at a specific price." [00:00-00:30]

### Support vs. Resistance: Definition

**Support Level** [02:30-03:30]:
- A price level where multiple reversals have started from similar prices in the past
- Historical and tested multiple times
- If price reversed multiple times from roughly the same point, it has a good chance of doing the same in the future
- Identified by looking for points where multiple reversals originated from similar prices

**Resistance Level** [02:30-03:30]:
- Mirror of support; a price level above current price where historical reversals have occurred
- Also based on multiple touches of previous levels

### Key Difference: Fresh vs. Historical

**Support/Resistance Trading** [02:00-04:30]:
- Based on historical, older, tested levels [02:00]
- Strength comes from multiple touches of the previous level [02:30]
- Works on round numbers, Fibonacci, Gann levels [04:00-04:30]
- Broken support becomes resistance and vice versa [04:30-05:00]
- "Support and resistance levels are somewhat riskier than trading supply and demand" [05:30-06:00]

**Supply/Demand Trading** [03:30-07:00]:
- Mainly look for **new zones** [03:30]
- "Fresh, untouched area where price spent last time" [03:30-04:00]
- "Don't wait for multiple retests of the same area. Several big candles and just one base candle could make a valid supply or demand zone" [04:00]
- Do NOT apply the "broken support becomes resistance" rule; "a supply zone which is broken doesn't turn into a demand zone" [05:00-05:30]
- "Supply and demand are fresh levels or zones where price has not been determined yet" [05:30-06:00]

**Advantage of Zones Over Levels** [05:30-07:00]:
- "Having a zone you can use to watch for entries is much easier than having a level" [06:00]
- "They offer you an area at which the market should reverse. You don't have to find a reversal signal forming at an exact point like you do when trading support and resistance levels. You have a whole zone you can use to place your trades. As long as the signal was found inside the supply or demand zone, it's a sign that a rejection could follow." [07:00]
- "Price will often fail to turn exactly on the level itself" when trading S/R, whereas zones give flexibility [06:00-06:30]

### Critical Insight: One-Time Use of Supply/Demand Zones

**Key Claim** [07:30-11:00]: Supply and demand zones work **only once**. After price returns to a zone and reverses, the zone loses effectiveness.

Rationale [08:00-10:30]:
- Zones form from smart money buying and selling [08:00-08:30]
- Price returns to zones because banks haven't placed all their trades or taken all profits [08:00-08:30]
- After first touch, banks have no reason to make price return [08:30]
- "Banks can get most of their trades placed creating the zone causing price to return and then reverse. Too many traders put too much attention to older supply and demand levels while the actual strength hides within fresh levels" [08:30-09:00]

Counterargument [09:00-10:30]: Some traders claim old zones perform as well as new ones; old zones do cause reversals months or years later. But speaker argues this doesn't make logical sense: "Why would they wait months or years to do this? If they want to get their remaining trades placed or take their profit, they'll make price come back to a zone quickly. They won't wait that long." [10:00-10:30]

**Time Factor** [10:30-11:30]:
- "Order flow will reposition itself over time. That is a statistical mathematical factor."
- "The fresher that supply or demand is, the more likely it is that order flow is still present in its previous form or new traders will react to it much more likely compared to any historical old price levels"
- "Always focus on what price structures are doing right now on your right side of the chart and not far back on the left side"
- "Fresh order flow will be in the newly formed structures"
- "Focus on the right side over the left side of the chart. Make this a priority. Sure, historical price action does matter, but near-term fresh price action always should have a priority" [11:30]

### Guidelines for Plotting Supply/Demand Zones

**Efficiency Rule** [12:00-12:30]:
- "Often, traders tend to pollute charts with 20 or 30 levels, where the whole meaning of anticipating the rejections will lose its value"
- "There is a low value in having a chart completely cluttered with levels"
- Keep chart clean and focused

**Key Variables for Zone Strength** [12:30-14:00]:

1. **First Retrace is Strongest** [12:30]: "First time retrace to the base is the strongest to enter. Always make sure the zone is still fresh, which means that after the initial creation of the zone, price has not returned to it yet." [12:30-13:00]

2. **Time at Zone** [13:00-13:30]: "Consider the time spent at the supply or demand level. How much time did the price spend at the zone? The less time price spends at the zone, the more out of balance supply and demand are at that price level. This means smart money are aggressively placing orders." [13:00-13:30]

3. **Distance Moved Away** [13:30]: "Analyze how far the price moved away from the zone. Look for extremely strong turning points. These are high probability price levels." [13:30]

4. **Recency** [13:30]: "Pay attention to the most recent zones. Have only the nearest ones in sight and keep your chart clean." [13:30]

5. **Volume** [14:00]: "Another variable to identify the strength of the supply or demand zone is the volume at which price formed the zone. The stronger the volume, the more important the zone could be once the price retests it." [14:00]

This is a theoretical argument not supported by empirical data or backtests in the video.

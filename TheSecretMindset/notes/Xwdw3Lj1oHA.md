## Moving Average Types Explained

### 1. Simple Moving Average (SMA)

- **Calculation**: Average of closing prices over a specified period
- **Characteristics**: Most common MA type [01:30]. More stable [01:30]. Signals price changes slowly [01:30]
- **Lag**: Greater lag compared to faster MAs [07:00]
- **False signals**: Fewer false signals [07:30]

### 2. Exponential Moving Average (EMA)

- **Characteristics**: Gives higher weighting to recent prices [02:00]. More sensitive to recent price dynamics [02:30]. More responsive than SMA [02:00]
- **Responsiveness**: Shorter EMA periods apply more weight to recent prices [02:00]
- **Calculation**: More complicated than SMA [02:30]
- **False signals**: More false signals than SMA [07:30]

### 3. Triple Exponential Moving Average (TEMA)

- **Calculation**: Triples the weighting of recent prices to reduce EMA lag [02:30-03:00]
- **Characteristics**: Responds to market movements quicker than SMA or EMA [03:00]

### 4. Adaptive Moving Average (AMA)

- **Calculation**: Multiplies EMA weighting by a volatility factor [03:00]
- **Characteristics**: Adapts to market changes and signals when volatility conditions change [03:00-03:30]. Filters noise and automatically changes speed based on market volatility [03:30]

### 5. Hull Moving Average (HMA)

- **Developer**: Alan Hull [03:30]
- **Characteristics**: Fast, responsive, reduced lag [03:30]. Uses weighted averages in calculation [03:30-04:00]
- **Claimed benefit**: Formula reduces lag and increases smoothness simultaneously [04:00]

### 6. Weighted Moving Average (WMA)

- **Characteristics**: Designed to find trends faster without whipsaws [04:00-04:30]. Offers more relevance on recent price moves [04:00-04:30]
- **Responsiveness**: Reacts more quickly to price movements than SMA or EMA [04:30]

### 7. Jurik Moving Average (JMA)

- **Use**: Employed by some institutional traders [04:30]
- **Characteristics**: Described by Jurik as a "powerful adaptive tracker" [04:30-05:00]
- **Claimed benefits**: Smooths time series data with very small lag, no overshoots, no oscillations [04:30-05:00]

## Moving Average Period Categories

### Long-Term Moving Averages

- **Periods**: 200 EMA, 365 EMA [05:30]
- **Most Common**: 200 EMA [05:30]
- **Application**: Often applied to daily charts [05:30]
- **Institutional Use**: Believed to be followed by banks, hedge funds, forex dealers [05:30-06:00]
- **Lag characteristic**: Includes market prices for past 200 periods; much greater lag than shorter MAs [07:00]

### Medium-Term Moving Averages

- **Periods**: 50 EMA, 100 EMA [06:00]
- **Most Common**: 50 EMA [06:00]
- **Characteristics**: Considered faster MA due to fewer input periods [06:00]. Reacts more to medium-term movements [06:00]
- **Uses**: One of the most effective trend indicators [06:00]. Offers dynamic support and resistance levels [06:00]

### Short-Term Moving Averages

- **Periods**: 10 EMA, 20 EMA [06:30]
- **Use Case**: Preferred by traders wanting to trade with current market momentum [06:30]
- **Responsiveness**: React fastest to price movements [06:30]

### Fibonacci-Based Moving Averages

- **Periods**: 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, 89, 144 [06:30-07:00]
- **Most Common**: 5 EMA, 8 EMA, 21 EMA, 55 EMA, 144 EMA [07:00]
- **Rationale**: Some traders derive EMA periods from Fibonacci sequence [06:30]

## Trade-off Between Lag and False Signals

The speaker notes a fundamental trade-off [07:00-07:30]:
- **Longer-term MAs (200)**: More lag, fewer false signals
- **Shorter-term MAs (10, 20)**: Faster response to price changes, more false signals
- **SMA**: Slower response, fewer false signals
- **EMA and WMA**: Faster response, more false signals

The speaker advises: "A trader must find a balance when using exponential moving averages" [07:30].

## Summary
A comprehensive framework for growing a small trading account from $100 through disciplined risk management, account phases, and psychological safeguards rather than a specific trading strategy. The video teaches three sequential phases of account growth: Phase 1 ($100-$250) focuses on survival, Phase 2 ($250-$500) on repeatability of edge, and Phase 3 ($500+) on scaling. Key principles include trading one setup/market/session, limiting to 1-2 trades per day, enforcing mechanical stop losses (circuit breakers, 80% daily range rule, 8-candle validity test), and measuring true expectation through formula-driven calculations. The emphasis is on trader psychology, discipline, and avoiding common behavioral traps (revenge trading, confidence-driven oversizing, premature breakeven moves).

## Core Discipline Rules

### Rule 1: One Setup, One Market, One Session [02:30 - 03:30]

"Trade on one setup, one market, one session. A small account can't validate five ideas at once. If you trade the euro-dollar at 8:00 using the MACD, try a quick scalp on gold at 10:00, and then trade a moving average crossover on Bitcoin after lunch, you're not doing yourself any favors" [02:30 - 03:00].

**Benefit** [03:00 - 03:30]: "The reframe is intentionally boring. Same market, same session, same setup until your eyes can't distinguish between a clean setup and a bad one" [03:00 - 03:30].

Example: "GBP-USD pair, 15-minute timeframe, London session... because it is global, liquid, and offers enough movement for day trading" [03:00 - 03:30].

### Rule 2: One or Two Trades Per Day Maximum [04:30 - 05:00]

"Your goal on a small account is one or two trades per day. You don't need 10 trades before lunch. Small accounts do not die for lack of patience. They die from lack of activity. Because of the belief that more screen time and more clicks equal faster growth. So, maximum one or two high-quality trades per day for 4 weeks" [04:30 - 05:00].

### Rule 3: Two-Loss Circuit Breaker [04:30 - 05:00]

"This rule comes with a safety switch. Two consecutive losses, or a 2% drop in your account in a single session. The laptop closes for the day. There's no going back" [04:30 - 05:00].

**Rationale** [05:00]: "I have seen traders blow up perfectly good accounts within 40 minutes of their second loss, trying to recoup their losses. The safety switch is a circuit breaker designed to protect you from the version of yourself that thinks the next click will fix everything" [05:00].

### Rule 4: 80% Daily Average Range Stop [05:30 - 06:00]

"Open the Daily Average Range indicator in TradingView. Once the price has moved 80% of that daily average, you're done. I don't care how clean the setup looks. By the time 80% of your daily range has been used, your next click may already be late. The price has already moved and you risk a late entry" [05:30 - 06:00].

**Binary rule** [06:00]: "Has the price moved more than 80% of its average daily range today? If so, close the platform. If not, you can search for your setup" [06:00].

### Rule 5: Eight-Candle Validity Test [08:30 - 09:30]

"Decide in advance how many candles the trade has to prove its validity. On the 15-minute chart, I assign eight candles to my setups. It's two hours of price action. If the price has not moved in my favor by the eighth candle, my reading was wrong and I close the position before the market charges me full price for the error" [08:30 - 09:00].

**Rule** [09:00 - 09:30]: "Has the trade moved at least half your risk distance in your favor within eight candles? If so, the operation remains open and you manage it. If not, and the price fluctuates around your entry point, you close it and accept the lower cost. Don't wait for the stop to make the decision for you" [09:00 - 09:30].

### Rule 6: Don't Move Stop to Breakeven Early [09:30 - 10:30]

"Moving your stop loss to breakeven too early gives you a sense of responsibility. It gives the feeling of protecting earnings. But over 50 or more trades, premature moves to breakeven will systematically cut into your profits because normal retracements prevent you from exiting trades that would have been successful. You move to breakeven, the price drops 10 pips, kicks you out, and then moves up 50 pips without you being in" [09:30 - 10:00].

**Data** [10:00 - 10:30]: "Backtests on trend-following entries show that traders who keep their original stops in place make 30% to 40% more profit than traders who trail them to breakeven" [10:00 - 10:30].

**Rule** [10:30]: "Don't touch your stop until the price has covered at least an hour in your favor. Below an hour of movement, the stop remains exactly where you placed it" [10:30].

### Rule 7: Position Sizing (0.5% Risk) [03:30 - 04:00]

"The position size is 0.5% of my $140 account, which means my total risk is about $0.70" [03:30 - 04:00].

## Expectation Calculation (Phase 3 Decision Gate) [11:00 - 11:30]

"Forget about the success rate. A 70% success rate means nothing if your average loss is three times your average win. The number that really matters is expectation" [11:00].

**Formula** [11:00 - 11:30]:
- (Average winning trade × Win rate) - (Average losing trade × Loss rate) = Expectation
- Positive = real advantage
- Zero or negative = donating to market

**Application frequency** [11:30]: "Apply this formula every 20 operations" [11:30].

**Decision rule** [11:30]: "Only one issue comes out. That single number is what decides whether you deserve stage three sizing. If you are truly ready to grow. Not your instinct. Not your profit and loss statement from last Tuesday" [11:30].

## Psychological Safeguard: Withdrawal Rule [11:30 - 12:30]

"When your account gets above $120, when you've cashed out your first 20% profit, withdraw it. Withdraw the money in cash. I don't care if it's 20 dollars" [11:30 - 12:00].

**Behavioral purpose** [12:00 - 12:30]: "That withdrawal turns a number on the screen into tangible proof of the ability to withdraw money from the market. Without it, the account looks like a video game. We act recklessly because the loss doesn't seem real. But once you have that $20 bill in your hand, every subsequent transaction takes on weight. It is the behavioral anchor that prevents you from treating the next 50 trades like a game" [12:00 - 12:30].

## Common Psychological Failures [06:30 - 08:30]

### Confidence-Driven Oversizing [06:30 - 07:00]
"At $190, after four consecutive wins, you feel like you've got it all figured out. So, you increase the position size and take the Grade B setup. Then the stop loss is hit and the account stops at $175" [06:30 - 07:00].

### Revenge Trading [07:00 - 07:30]
"Your next operation is not scheduled. It's revenge. You enter because the loss seems unfair to you, because the graph looks like it owes you something. And this time you lose" [07:00 - 07:30].

### Recovery Betting [07:30 - 08:00]
"Then you double the size because the mental math says that a win brings you back to $200. Only your judgment is impaired. And those calculations are a fantasy you're telling yourself. The bill drops to $100. You lost everything you gained in 4 weeks in 1 hour" [07:30 - 08:00].

### Temporal Failure [08:00 - 08:30]
"There's a name for what derailed that first operation: it's called 'temporal failure.' And spotting it early changes the loss completely. Decide in advance how many candles the trade has to prove its validity" [08:00 - 08:30].

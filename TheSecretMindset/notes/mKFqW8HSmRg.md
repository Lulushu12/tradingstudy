## Summary
A doji trading strategy that uses doji candlestick patterns formed near support/resistance levels (particularly pivot points and Fibonacci levels) as entry signals. Doji candles indicate market indecision and a potential momentum change. The key is not to trade doji in isolation but to wait for confirmation: price must close beyond the doji's high (for longs) or low (for shorts) in the following 1-3 candles. The speaker interprets doji as continuation setups, not reversals, and combines them with other technical tools for confirmation. Five types of doji are identified with different characteristics and reliability.

## Doji Candlestick Pattern Characteristics

**Basic Definition:** Open price and close price are same or very close [00:00].

**Interpretation:** Suggests lack of control by either bulls or bears [02:30]; market indecision [02:00]; possible early warning of momentum/direction change [03:00]. However, "does not guarantee that a certain setup will occur or that the market will reverse" [03:30].

**Five Types of Doji:** [05:00]-[08:00]

1. **Common Doji:** Open ≈ close; equal-length upper and lower shadows [05:00]

2. **Gravestone Doji:** Open and close at/near low; long upper shadow [05:30]; bearish pattern indicating failed rally [05:30]. "Not profitable when traded by itself" [06:00]; needs strong support/resistance [06:00].

3. **Dragonfly Doji:** Open and close at/near high; long lower shadow [06:00]; bullish pattern showing bulls pushed price up despite selling pressure [06:30]. "Not profitable when traded by itself" [07:00]; needs strong support/resistance [07:00].

4. **Long-legged Doji:** Long upper and lower shadows; reflects strong indecision [07:30]; "offers many false signals and should only be traded on higher time frames and in combination with other technical tools" [07:30].

5. **Rare Doji:** Open = high = low = close [08:00]; indicates "complete and total uncertainty" [08:00]; very illiquid markets or data issues [08:00].

**Relevance Determination:** If recent candles all have small real bodies (open ≈ close), do not consider new small-bodied candle as doji [01:00]. Follow recent price action context [00:30].

## Strategy 1: Doji Near Support/Resistance with Confirmation

### Indicators and settings
- Doji candlestick pattern (any of the five types) [05:00]
- Support/Resistance levels for location:
  - Pivot points (speaker's preference) [08:30]
  - Recent market swings [08:30]
  - Fibonacci retracement levels [08:30]
  - Round numbers [04:30]
  - Previous support/resistance levels [04:30]
- Optional: Additional technical indicators for confirmation
  - Stochastic [09:30]
  - Moving average [09:30]
  - Other trader preference [09:30]

### Context / bias filter
Doji must form at "relevant market highs or lows" [04:30]. When doji forms in middle of trend or range, has "little significance" [04:30].

"Always take trades only around support and resistance areas" [08:30]. Best when occurs near pivot points [08:30].

Doji combined with other technical signals at support/resistance more significant [01:30].

Common doji more reliable than long-legged doji [07:30].

### Entry trigger
**Bullish Entry (Long):**
1. Common or Dragonfly doji forms at/near support level (pivot point, Fibonacci, previous support) [08:30]
2. Next 1-3 candles must close above the high of doji [08:30]
3. Confirmation occurs when close is above doji's high [09:00]
4. Example: close above doji high, plus close above moving average [10:00]

**Bearish Entry (Short):**
1. Common or Gravestone doji forms at/near resistance level (pivot point, previous resistance) [08:30]
2. Next 1-3 candles must close below the low of doji [08:30]
3. Confirmation occurs when close is below doji's low [09:00]

**Critical rule:** "The next one to three candles after a doji forms must close above the high of that doji candle. That is the key; the close is the confirmation that a bullish transition took place" [08:30].

### Stop loss
Initially: "Use stop below the low of the doji" [09:00]

As trade moves profitable: "Once the market begins to produce a profit and moves in the desired direction, then you can change it or continually trail the stop" [09:00]

### Take profit / exit
Trail stop as market moves in desired direction [09:00]. No specific profit targets given; implied to follow trend until stopped out.

### Invalidation / skip conditions
1. Doji formed in middle of trend or range (not near support/resistance) [04:30]
2. Close does not go above/below doji high/low within 1-3 candles [09:00]
3. Long-legged doji on lower timeframes [07:30]
4. Gravestone or Dragonfly doji not at strong support/resistance [06:00], [07:00]
5. Doji traded in isolation without other technical tools [02:30], [09:30]

### Claimed performance
Shows multiple worked examples [10:30] where doji formed near pivot levels and following candle(s) closed beyond doji high, resulting in trades that ran in predicted direction.

Notes: One gravestone example shows "price indeed declined after the appearance of a gravestone doji, but not before taking some stop-losses on its way" [06:00], emphasizing importance of proper location and other confirmations.

## Key Strategy Principles

**Doji as reversal vs continuation:** Speaker explicitly states preference: "I prefer to consider doji candles as accumulation zones where bull or bears are gaining momentum for a continuation of the main trend. I'm not chasing reversals like most of traders, I'm looking for continuation patterns" [11:00].

**Never trade in isolation:** "Use doji candle in combination with other technical indicators or price action, never on its own" [02:30]. "It's important to trade this pattern near strong support and resistance levels and in combination with other tools" [06:00], [07:00].

**Confirmation is essential:** "A doji candle by itself is not significant enough to predict a reversal...it represents only a warning of an impending trend change" [03:30]. Next candle close is "the key" confirmation [08:30].

**Comparison to author Steve Nison:** "Doji candles tend to be better at indicating a change in trend when they occur at market highs instead of at market lows" [04:00]. Explained by market dynamics: uptrends require new buying power to continue, while downtrends can continue at full strength without weakening [04:00].

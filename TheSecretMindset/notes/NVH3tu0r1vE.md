## Summary
This video teaches price action trading using line charts, a simple technical tool that displays only closing prices to filter market noise. The video covers four main approaches: trend line breakouts, chart pattern recognition, support and resistance trading, and combining line charts with moving averages. Line charts are presented as a "simple method" used by early technical analysts and as a validation tool for candlestick-based strategies.

## Notable claims and caveats

- Line charts are presented as filtering noise by showing only closing prices: "A line chart is the simplest type of chart, representing the trading instrument's closing price on each day" [01:00]
- Drawing trend lines "is quite subjective, is not a precise science" [02:30]
- Speaker notes: "It's easy to read a line chart, looking at past history, but in real time, reading price action and trying to predict the next price movements is not an easy job" [04:30]
- Line charts are difficult to backtest: "trading strategies are very hard to be back tested by using a simple line chart" [04:30]
- Line charts are underused: "the line charts are the least likely to be used by retail traders" [09:00]
- Recommended as validation tool: "next time when you are in doubt and you don't know if a trade setup is valid or not, open a second window and take a look at the same image, but on a line chart. It will save you a lot of losses in the long run." [09:30]
- No discussion of transaction costs, spreads, slippage, or commissions
- Charles Dow (founder of Dow Theory) focused only on closing prices, as he "believed that plotting a record of highs and lows tends to obscure the real value" [02:00]

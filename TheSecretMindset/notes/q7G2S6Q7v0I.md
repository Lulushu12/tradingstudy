## Summary
This video explains tick charts as an alternative to time-based charts for day trading and scalping. Bars form based on a fixed number of transactions (not time), removing time as a variable and reducing market noise. The speaker covers seven advantages: (1) less noise, (2) better volatility analysis, (3) clearer swings, (4) faster breakout identification, (5) more accurate indicator signals, (6) better volume measurement, (7) tighter stop-loss placement. No complete mechanizable strategy is presented; tick charts are positioned as a complementary tool to time-based charts.

## Tick chart basics

### What is a tick chart [00:30-01:00]
- Bars plotted based on particular number of transactions [00:30]
- Example: 200-tick chart plots new bar after 200 trades completed [00:30]
- Single transaction = 1 contract or 50 contracts; number of trades (not contracts) determines bar creation [01:00]
- Tick charts do NOT measure true number of contracts, only number of transactions [01:00]

### Tick chart settings [01:00-02:00]

**Common settings by trading style [01:30-02:00]**
- Scalping: 50 or 100 ticks [01:30]
- Day trading: 500 or 1000 ticks most common [02:00]
- Alternative: use Fibonacci numbers (21, 34, 55, 89, 144) [01:30]
- Comparison approach: choose tick number similar to preferred time-based chart [01:30]

"There is no best number of ticks. Just test different settings and select one that suits you" [02:00]

## Seven advantages of tick charts

### 1. Less Market Noise [02:00-03:00]
- Transaction-based; new bars only when enough trades occur
- Eliminates "useless candles with no real value" [02:30]
- Reduces wicks that induce trader error [02:30]
- "Higher the noise, more difficult to make intelligent decisions" [02:30]
- Compresses low-activity periods: time-chart shows 30-40 useless candles, tick chart shows 10-15 relevant ones [02:30-03:00]

### 2. Better Volatility Analysis [03:00-04:00]
- New bar only when enough trades between buyers/sellers
- High activity = more bars plotted, signals increased volatility [03:00]
- Example: market open = high volatility, quick tick bars; lunch hour = low volatility, fewer bars [03:30]
- "Better measurement of volatility because time-based charts show less important candles" [03:30]
- Application: trader can skip signals on low-volatility days [04:00]

### 3. Clearer Swings [04:00-04:30]
- Without accumulation of small candles, easier to spot swings [04:30]
- During high volatility, one long time-based candle shows as several smaller tick candles [04:30]
- Tick charts provide more information about recent market swings [04:30]
- Example: non-farm payroll 3-wave downtrend showed clearly on 50-tick vs 4 red candles with wicks on 1-min [05:00]

### 4. Better Breakout Visualization [05:00-05:30]
- Identify breakouts faster than time-based charts [05:30]
- Enter positions faster at better price levels [05:30]
- Example: tick chart showed retest after breakout while time-based chart didn't [06:00]
- Difference between successful trade and missed opportunity [06:00]

### 5. More Accurate Indicator Signals [06:00-07:00]
- Market noise reduced, so indicator signals have more relevance [06:30]
- Example: Stochastic divergence visible on 200-tick chart but not on 5-minute chart [06:30-07:00]
- Choppiness more visible on time-based charts [07:00]
- Tick chart signals have "slight difference" showing more clarity [07:00]

### 6. Better Volume Measurement [07:00-07:30]
- Volume indicator on tick chart shows better understanding of price move strength/weakness [07:30]
- Combining volume + tick charts helps join strong moves and avoid weak ones [07:30]
- "Each tick bar is equal" - allows seeing which movements have high volume [07:30]
- Tells which trends supported by smart money vs just retail [07:30]

### 7. Smaller, More Accurate Stop-Loss Orders [07:30-09:00]
- Especially useful during high volatility and rapid movements [08:00]
- Rapid moves plot multiple price swings usable for stops/profits [08:00]
- Time-based chart may use only 1-2 bars for stop placement [08:00]
- Tick chart shows more waves and entry points for better stop placement [08:00]
- Example: scalping up-move required 30-pip stop on 1-minute, but 20-pip stop on 50-tick chart [08:30-09:00]
- 10-pip difference "huge" for scalping [09:00]

## Limitations and caveats [09:00-09:30]

"Tick charts come at a cost as not many platforms offer free tick data" [09:00-09:30]
Quality tick data expensive and not always 100% accurate [09:00]
Tick chart data varies between feeds; some contain errors and miss ticks [09:30]
"May affect your results. Sometimes drastically" [09:30]

## Recommended use [09:30-10:00]

"Use tick charts as complementary tool, not exclusively" [09:30]
Recommended approach: spot trends and S/R on time-based charts, pinpoint entries on tick charts [10:00]
This combination: reduces noise, determines volatility, takes signals on tick charts while confirming with time-based [10:00]

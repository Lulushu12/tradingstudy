## ATR Indicator Explanation

### Calculation [00:00-01:00]

"The ATR is measured as the greatest of any of the following 3 metrics:
- the value of the current high minus the low
- the distance of the current high minus the previous close
- the distance of the current low minus the previous close" [00:00-00:30]

"The average true range (ATR) is basically an exponential moving average of the true range" [01:00].

### Interpretation [01:00-03:30]

**What ATR Measures [00:00-01:30]:**
"The average true range measures the price range of an asset – the higher the volatility the higher the ATR" [00:00].

"The ATR indicator moves up and down as price movements become larger or smaller" [00:30-01:00].

**Common Settings [01:30]:**
"Most common settings of this indicator are the 14-day ATR" [01:30].

**Period Adjustment [01:30-02:30]:**
"Longer ATR will be slower and will generate fewer trading signals. Shorter ATR will generate a higher number of trading signals and will increase trading activity" [01:30-02:00].

"An average true range of only 5 periods would not offer a large enough sample to generate an accurate indication of the true range of the security. An average true range of 50 periods would generate a more smooth average true range on the chart, with fewer and possibly high-quality signals" [01:30-02:00].

**Timeframe Dependence [02:00-02:30]:**
"ATR's value differs from time frame to time frame, is not a unique value" [02:30]. Example: "If you're trading on the daily chart, the ATR indicator will indicate the average volatility over the past 50 days. However, if you're trading on the H4 chart, the ATR indicator will display the average volatility over the past 50 H4 candles" [02:00-02:30].

**What ATR Does NOT Measure [02:30-03:30]:**
"ATR does not measure the market direction. By tracking the average true range, a trader cannot identify the prevailing trend on the market. The ATR only tracks the magnitude of range, so it has limited use for generating accurate trading signals" [02:30-03:30].

## Use Case 1: Filtering Trades Based on Volatility

### Strategy Concept [03:00-04:00]

"ATR represents a range indicator for filtering out trades. If you prefer volatile markets or if your trading strategy is based on huge market moves, then ATR can filter out markets that are low in volatility" [02:30-03:00].

"Even if you prefer calm, quiet markets the average true range indicator will help you to filter out those periods of times that you would prefer to avoid" [03:00].

### Example: High Price Movement After Average Range [03:30-04:00]

"Assume a stock moves $1 a day, on average. There is no significant news out, but price has already moved let's say 30% more than the average, and now you're getting a buy signal from a strategy. The buy signal may be valid but, since the price has already moved significantly more than average, betting that the price will continue to go up and expand the range even further may not be a prudent decision" [03:30-04:00].

"Since the price is already up substantially and has moved more than the average, the price is more likely to fall and stay within the price range already established...shorting is probably a good option, assuming a valid sell signal occurs" [04:00].

### Critical Rule [04:00-04:30]

"Entries and exits should not be based on the ATR alone. The ATR is a tool that should be used in conjunction with a strategy to help filtering trades" [04:00-04:30].

Example: "You shouldn't sell simply because the price has moved up and the daily range is larger than usual. You short only if a valid sell signal occurs, based on your particular strategy" [04:00-04:30].

## Use Case 2: Setting Profit Targets

### Method [04:30-05:00]

"Knowing the average range is extremely useful since it allows the trader to estimate how much profit potential there is in the market. For example, there is no point looking for 200 pips of profit from a trade in a currency pair, if the average true range for that market over the last 14 days is only 60 points" [04:30-05:00].

"A simpler solution is to halve the 14-day ATR of the instrument and use this figure as your profit target. Thus, after entering a trade in, you could set yourself a profit target of around 30 points" [04:30-05:00].

### Formula

**Profit Target = (14-day ATR) / 2**

Example: If 14-day ATR = 60 pips, target = 30 pips.

## Use Case 3: Identifying Low Volatility Zones (Potential Setup Areas)

### Strategy [05:30-06:30]

"A viable strategy would be to search for low volatility levels, in order to find a trade in the direction of the main trend" [05:30].

"Keep in mind that the low volatility itself is not enough for a new trend. This translates into the fact that the current trend has run out of steam, it doesn't tell us that a new trend has begun. So, we must use the Average True Range in conjunction with other oscillators or price action" [06:00-06:30].

### Example Entry Confirmation [06:00-06:30]

"In this example we identified low levels of volatility, but we cannot enter the market blindly. A smarter approach would be to wait for the average true range to exit from that level and confirm the volatility increase with a trend line breakout on the chart, or a rising Stochastic for example, or a divergence on the RSI or CCI, depending on your strategy" [06:00-06:30].

## Use Case 4: Trailing Stop Loss Placement

### Method [06:30-07:30]

"A trailing stop loss is a way to exit a trade if the asset price moves against you but also enables you to move the exit point if the price is moving in your favor" [06:30-07:00].

**Stop Loss Placement [07:00-07:30]:**
"At the time of a trade, look at the current ATR reading. A rule of thumb is to multiply the ATR by two to determine a reasonable stop loss point" [07:00].

"If you're buying a stock, you might place a stop loss at a level twice the ATR below the entry price. If you're shorting a stock, you would place a stop loss at a level twice the ATR above the entry price" [07:00-07:30].

### Trailing Mechanism [07:00-07:30]

"If you're long and the price moves favorably, continue to move the stop loss to twice the ATR below the price. In this scenario, the stop loss only moves up, not down. Once it is moved up, it stays there until it can be moved up again or the trade is closed as a result of the price dropping to hit the trailing stop loss level" [07:00-07:30].

"The same process works for short trades, only in that case, the stop loss only moves down" [07:30].

### Example [07:30-08:00]

"Say you take a long trade at $10 and the ATR is $0.10. You would place a stop loss at $9.80 (the 10 cents ATR multiplied by 2). The price rises to $10.20, and the ATR remains at $0.10. The trailing stop loss is now moved up to $10. When the price moves up to $10.50, the stop loss moves up to $10.30, locking in at least a 30 cent profit on the trade" [07:30-08:00].

## Use Case 5: ATR Bands for Visual Stop and Target Placement

### Overview [08:00-09:00]

"In order to make it easier to visually see where those stops would be, you could use the ATR bands. This indicator will show an upper and lower band around the price, showing where you can put your stops (or trail them in open positions)" [08:00-08:30].

**Three Uses [08:30-09:00]:**
- "It's an easy visual way to see where you can place your stop loss"
- "You can use a multiple of ATR as your target"
- "You can trail your stops alongside the ATR bands" [08:30]

### ATR Bands Parameters [08:30-09:30]

"ATR bands indicator has only 2 variables: the period and the multiplier" [08:30].

**Period Setting [08:30-09:00]:**
"By default, ATR period is set to 24 with the rationale that one day has 24 hours so that way, we're getting the average price movement for the past day. However, if you're day trading, it could be useful to have a shorter ATR period, for example if you want to make recent price volatility more relevant" [08:30-09:00].

**Multiplier Setting [09:00-09:30]:**
"ATR multiplier also depends on your trading style. Let's say your current ATR value is 20 pips and you use a multiplier of 2, then the bands will be 40 pips on each side. If you use a multiplier of 3, the bands will be 60 pips on each side" [09:00-09:30].

### Customization Rule [09:30-10:00]

"There is no universal rule for setting stops according to the ATR. It depends on each trader's individual risk tolerance while also allowing the trade 'breathing room' to develop. So backtest and choose the settings that are in line with your strategy" [09:30-10:00].

## Important Caveats and Limitations

### ATR Does Not Signal Direction [03:30-04:00]

"The ATR does not measure the market direction...ATR only tracks the magnitude of range, so it has limited use for generating accurate trading signals. It's a useful indicator only for giving an idea about how much the price may move in a defined time frame" [03:30].

### ATR Requires Strategy Confirmation [04:00-04:30]

"Entries and exits should not be based on the ATR alone. The ATR is a tool that should be used in conjunction with a strategy to help filtering trades" [04:00-04:30].

### Low Volatility Does Not Predict Trend [06:00-06:30]

"Low volatility itself is not enough for a new trend...it doesn't tell us that a new trend has begun" [06:00-06:30]. Require "a trend line breakout on the chart, or a rising Stochastic for example, or a divergence on the RSI or CCI" for confirmation [06:00-06:30].

### Summary of Limitations [10:00]

"Because the ATR does not measure direction and simply considers the magnitude of range, it has limited utility as a means for generating trading signals. But it is a useful tool for providing an idea about how much a market may move and will help you to apply a proper position size and a proper stop placement" [10:00].

## Summary
A psychological analysis of position sizing and its effect on trading execution and discipline. The speaker argues that most trading psychology advice (meditation, journaling, mindset work) misses the core issue: position size determines whether the brain operates in "planning mode" (executing your strategy) or "survival mode" (fear-driven decision making). When position size exceeds an individual's "override threshold," the brain automatically switches to survival mode, causing hesitation on entries, stop loss moving, early profit-taking, and revenge trading. The video teaches four binary rules to identify when position sizing is too large and recommends finding the specific risk percentage where all trading behavior becomes automatic and emotionless.

## Position Sizing and Override Threshold Framework

This is not a trading strategy but a position sizing discipline system. The speaker's core thesis:

"When your position size crosses a certain line, discipline stops being something you can choose" [02:00].

### The Override Threshold Concept

**Definition:** The position size where brain switches from "planning mode" to "survival mode" [02:30]. Below threshold, trader follows system; above threshold, survival mode drives decisions [02:30].

**Manifestation:** Survival mode feels like "smart decisions" [03:00], not panic. It "whispers reasons" and "builds cases" [03:00]. Sounds like experience but is actually fear disguised [03:00].

**Threshold varies by trader:** New traders might hit it at 1% risk; experienced traders at 3-5% [03:00]. "Everyone has one" [03:00].

### The Four Binary Rules for Identifying Oversized Risk

**Rule 1: Entry Hesitation** [05:00]
- **Indicator:** If entering the setup triggers negotiation or hesitation instead of automatic click
- **Diagnosis:** Position size is past the line [05:00]
- **Examples of override signals:**
  - Inner voice says "Wait for one more candle" [04:30]
  - Questioning whether you're "sure about this setup" [04:30]
  - Pause before clicking entry [04:30]
- **Solution:** Drop position size until click is automatic, "no voice inside your head, no pause. Just a simple click of a button" [05:00]
- **Result of ignoring:** Entry becomes late (20+ pips worse entry), stop distance worsens, position fails [04:30]

**Rule 2: Stop Loss Movement** [07:00]
- **Indicator:** Urge to move stop wider after entering trade
- **When it occurs:** When market pushes back against position; price starts moving toward stop [05:30]
- **Psychological mechanism:** Size turns a "question" into a "threat"; brain cannot let threat land [06:00]; creates stories ("I still believe in this trade," "I'm being flexible") [06:30]
- **Diagnosis:** Size crossed override threshold [07:00]. Not about stop placement being wrong [07:00]
- **Cost:** Planned small loss (20 pips) becomes large loss (140 pips) [06:30]
- **Solution:** Reduce position size until approaching stop "feels like a question, not a threat" [12:00]

**Rule 3: Early Profit-Taking** [08:30]
- **Indicator:** Urge to close profitable trade before target
- **Psychological mechanism:** Open profit feels like "something you own," "something fragile to grab instead of a process" [08:00]; sounds like "smart traders protect profits" [08:00]
- **Disguise:** Feels like experienced trader making sharp call; actually survival mode [08:30]
- **Example:** 1:3.6 risk-reward setup with 50 pips open profit closed at 51 pips instead of 180-pip target [08:00]
- **Diagnosis:** Position size creates anxiety about possession, not confidence in plan [08:30]
- **Solution:** Reduce size until open profit feels like "watching the plan work, not something you need to protect" [08:30]

**Rule 4: Revenge Trading** [11:00]
- **Indicator:** Urge to immediately re-enter after a loss
- **When it occurs:** In the 90 seconds after a trade closes [10:00]
- **Psychological mechanism:** Loss feels like "blood"; creates urge to get money back immediately [09:30]; feels like "energy," "momentum," "resilience," not fear [10:30]
- **Escalation pattern:** After first loss, switch to faster timeframe (1-minute instead of 4-hour), double size on next trade, ignore structure [09:30]
- **Danger:** The most destructive mask; blew author's first account in single session [09:00]
- **Diagnosis:** Override running at full speed with nothing to contain it [11:00]
- **Solution:** After any loss, wait 30 minutes before next trade [11:00]. If 30-minute wait feels impossible, the loss was oversized [11:00]

### Core Finding: Historical Performance Gap

Speaker tested strategy on backtest: 62% win rate over 8 months [03:30]. Same system live trading: 31% win rate [03:30]. 

Root cause: All 93 live trades were entered late, not wrong entries [03:30]. "Every single time I entered late, my position size was over a number I hadn't noticed yet" [03:30].

Speaker's conclusion: "I thought I had a focus problem. Actually, I had a size problem" [04:00].

### The Solution: One Specific Risk Percentage

Speaker recommends finding "a boring number" - a specific risk percentage where [12:00]:
- Entry click is automatic
- Stop approaching feels like a question, not a threat
- Open profit feels like watching plan unfold
- Closing at stop feels like finishing trade, not losing argument
- Every trade makes you feel nothing (no hope, dread, excitement, chest tightness) [12:00]

This "boring number" is different for each trader [12:00] but once found, should "lock it and never cross it" [12:00].

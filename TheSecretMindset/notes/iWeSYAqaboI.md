This video teaches supply and demand zone trading, a framework for identifying institutional order imbalances. The strategy focuses on finding "fresh, unmitigated" zones where price moved explosively and quickly, indicating significant order imbalance. ### Entry trigger

**Identifying the zone location:**
1. Spot explosive price movements (strong rally followed by sudden drop, or steep decline followed by rapid bounce) [04:00]
2. Mark the origin of that move as the potential supply or demand zone [04:30]

**Zone types:**

1. **Base/Range zones:** Price consolidating in tight range, then breaking out. Edges of the range are supply/demand levels [04:30]

2. **Pivot zones:** Form at key turning points (swing highs/lows) with distinct candle characteristics [05:00]:
   - Demand pivot: Strong bearish candle where next candle closes back above its high, showing shift from selling to buying [05:00]
   - Supply pivot: Strong bullish candle where next candle closes below its low, showing shift from buying to selling [05:00]

**Entry methods [14:30-15:30]:**

1. **Aggressive:** Set limit order directly on the zone level, assuming zone holds [14:30]

2. **Conservative:** Wait for confirmation candle pattern at the level (e.g., bullish reversal pattern like pin bar or engulfing candle at demand zone) [15:00]

3. **Multi-timeframe:** Identify zone on higher timeframe (e.g., 1-hour), then drop to lower timeframe (e.g., 15-minute) to find reversal pattern (double bottom, change of character) for tighter entry [15:30]

## Key Concepts and Principles

**Supply and Demand Zones Definition [00:00-01:00]:**
- Form where there's significant imbalance between buyers and sellers
- Demand zone: More buyers than sellers
- Supply zone: More sellers than buyers

**Why Institutions Use These Zones [03:00-03:30]:**
Institutions (banks, hedge funds) have massive orders (millions/billions) that cannot execute all at once without moving the market. They gradually build/unwind positions at supply and demand zones to minimize price impact.

**Time Spent in Zone [08:30]:**
The less time price spends in a zone before making a strong move, the more out-of-balance supply and demand are, and the higher probability of successful trade on return [08:30].

**Fresh vs. Mitigated Zones [09:00-09:30]:**
- Fresh/unmitigated: NOT been tested or absorbed
- Mitigated: Already tested multiple times; gets weaker with each test [09:00]
- Multiple tests can absorb pending orders, reducing imbalance extremity [09:00]
- Generally prioritize fresh zones [09:30]

**Flip Zones [10:30-11:00]:**
A previous supply zone becomes a demand zone (or vice versa) after price breaks through decisively and closes on the other side. This represents a shift in control and the zone now acts as support (if it was supply and got flipped) [10:30].

**Inducement [11:00-12:00]:**
The presence of liquidity in front of a zone (buy orders stacked up at demand zones, sell orders at supply zones). Without inducement, institutions are less likely to be interested in the level [11:30]. Inducement can be in form of swing low or consolidation pattern [11:30].

**Support/Resistance vs. Supply/Demand [12:30-14:00]:**
- Support/resistance: Levels where price has reversed or consolidated multiple times in past
- Supply/demand zones: Fresh, unmitigated levels with recent order imbalance
- Support/resistance trading assumes past levels hold in future
- Supply/demand trading looks for new, untested zones with significant order flow [13:00]
- Once a fresh demand zone gets tested and holds, it becomes established support [13:30]
- Probability of reaction diminishes with each subsequent test [13:30]

**Zone Strength Assessment [16:00-18:30]:**
1. Speed of move away from zone: Faster move = stronger zone [16:00]
2. Time spent at zone: Less time = stronger zone [16:30]
3. Size of move: Extended move in opposite direction = significant zone [17:00]
4. Volume at zone: Noticeable uptick indicates institutional activity [17:30]
5. Break of market structure: Zone causing break of structure = stronger [18:00]

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Trading from weak structure** - Zooming in too much and losing sight of overall trend [18:30-19:00]
2. **Wrong expectations** - Expecting full trend reversal from old, tested zones [19:30]
3. **Trading with no inducement** - No liquidity stacked in front of zone [19:30-20:00]
4. **Trading old zones as if they're fresh** - Not recognizing that tested zones have lost their imbalance [09:00]

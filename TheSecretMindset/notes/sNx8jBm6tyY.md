## Foundational Framework: Four Candlestick Types

The video categorizes all candlesticks into four types based on high/low relationships to the previous bar, not on closing price:

### Type 1: Up Bars [02:00-02:30]

Definition: Higher high, higher low compared to the previous bar [02:00].

Example: A red candle that closes lower than it opened but still has a higher high and higher low than the previous bar is classified as an up bar, not a down bar [02:30].

### Type 2: Down Bars [02:00-02:30]

Definition: Lower high, lower low compared to the previous bar [02:00].

### Type 3: Inside Bars [02:00-02:30]

Definition: Lower high, higher low relative to the previous bar [02:00]. If the high or low (or both) are equal to the previous bar's, it is still considered an inside bar [02:30].

Implication: "Inside bars do not affect the direction of the current swing because they do not break the previous high and low" [04:30-05:00].

### Type 4: Outside Bars [02:30-03:00]

Definition: Higher high, lower low relative to the previous bar [02:30]. If either the high or low is equal to the previous bar's, it is still an outside bar [02:30-03:00].

Implication: "Outside bars break both the previous high and low so it introduces uncertainty into the market structure. However, as each swing is a minor trend itself and trends tend to continue, we give the benefit of the doubt to the current swing. Thus, outside bars in an upswing continues the upswing. Similarly, outside bars in a downswing continues the downswing" [04:30-05:00].

## Market Swing Identification

### Swing Rules [04:30-05:30]

1. "An up bar starts an upswing and confirms the end of a downswing" [04:30]
2. "A down bar starts a downswing and confirms the end of an upswing" [04:30]
3. "Inside bars do not affect the direction of the current swing" [04:30]
4. "Outside bars...in an upswing continue the upswing. Similarly, outside bars in a downswing continue the downswing" [04:30-05:00]

### Exceptional Cases [05:30-06:30]

"Under exceptional circumstances, it is possible for a swing to form without either an up bar or a down bar" [05:30]. This occurs when "the market rises above the last swing high without forming an up bar" [05:30]. "An outside bar is always involved in such cases" [05:30-06:00].

Example: A bullish outside bar can break above the last swing high without an up bar, signaling a new upswing [06:00-06:30]. Conversely, a bearish outside bar can create a new downswing without a down bar [06:00-06:30].

## Swing Pivots and Support/Resistance

### Swing Pivot Definition [07:00-07:30]

"Swing pivots are the turning points of each market swing. The point where an upswing turns down is a swing high. The point where a downswing turns up is a swing low" [07:00-07:30].

### Why Swing Pivots Matter [07:30-08:30]

"Swing pivots are price points that turned the tide of the market. These points are not random. They represent a momentary change in demand and supply forces" [07:30].

**Swing Highs as Resistance [07:30-08:00]:**
"In this example, bulls could not move the market above this swing high, not even for one tick. This means that at that point in time, no one was willing to offer a price higher than the swing high" [07:30-08:00]. "When prices move close to or above a swing high, we must remember that traders saw no value in buying above that point previously. Assuming that most traders have not changed their opinions, prices are unlikely to move above the swing high" [08:00-08:30]. "The swing high marks a price area that resists the market from moving up. This is what we call a resistance area" [08:00].

**Swing Lows as Support [08:00-08:30]:**
"In the case of a swing low, the bears could not push prices below it. This occurrence implies that no trader was willing to sell below that particular price point" [08:00-08:30]. "Traders perceived value below the swing low. They want to buy value and not sell it. So when everyone wants to buy and no one wants to sell, price goes up and we get a swing low" [08:30]. "When the market falls near a swing low, assuming that traders still see value in buying below the swing low, prices are unlikely to fall below it. The swing low supports the market and is a support area" [08:30-09:00].

### Failed Support and Resistance [09:00-09:30]

"Failed resistance turns into support. Failed support turns into resistance" [09:00].

"When a resistance level projected from a swing high fails to block a market from rising higher, it turns into a support area. This is because, despite its failure, the price point remains memorable to market participants. A price point that has failed decisively as a resistance will then serve as a reliable support" [09:00-09:30].

Example: Failed resistance becomes a powerful support zone [09:30].

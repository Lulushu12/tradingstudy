## Summary

This video presents three TICK Index scalping strategies for intraday trading. TICK measures the difference between NYSE stocks trading on upticks versus downticks, reflecting short-term market sentiment. The three approaches are: (1) TICK Range Trading—entering at overbought/oversold extremes (+1000/-1000) when price tests support/resistance levels; (2) TICK Divergences—shorting when price makes higher highs but TICK makes lower highs, or going long when price makes lower lows but TICK makes higher lows; and (3) Zero-Line Crossing—buying pullbacks in uptrends when TICK crosses below zero (confirmed with Fibonacci/pivots), or selling bounces in downtrends when TICK crosses above zero. All three strategies emphasize using TICK in conjunction with price levels and other technical confirmations, not as a standalone signal.

## Strategy 1: TICK Range Trading at Key Levels

### Indicators and settings
- TICK Index values [03:00-03:30]:
  - Neutral: +200 to -300
  - Bullish: > +200
  - Bearish: < -300
  - Very bullish: > +500
  - Very bearish: < -500
  - Extreme: > +1000 or < -1000
- Support/Resistance levels: horizontal lines at swing highs/lows [04:30-05:00]

### Context / bias filter
[04:00-04:30] "TICK overbought and oversold levels can vary depending on conditions. A reading above +500 indicates an overbought condition and a reading below –500 indicates an oversold market. Readings above +1000 or below –1000 are extreme conditions and we will try to concentrate our attention on these levels."

Focus on TICK extremes (±1000) at price levels only.

### Entry trigger
[04:00-05:00] Entry at overbought/oversold levels when price tests support/resistance:
"A simple trading approach is to place trades when the TICK signals an overbought or oversold market as price is testing the support or resistance levels of a trading range."

Example short entry [04:30-05:00]:
"As the market traded into this resistance zone, the TICK moved above +1000, signaling an overbought market. Price then rejected that area, and the TICK indicator turned down. A short sale would have been placed when price dropped back below the resistance level."

### Stop loss
[05:00] "A protective stop would be placed just above the high of rejection, which is the top of this swing move."

For shorts: Stop above the resistance level / swing high.
For longs: Stop below the support level / swing low (implied inverse).

## Strategy 2: TICK Divergences

### Indicators and settings
- TICK Index: same levels as Strategy 1 [03:00-03:30]
- Price action: swing highs and lows
- Trendline: optional confirmation [07:30]
- Key levels: support/resistance [07:00]

### Context / bias filter
[06:00] Divergence concept: "Divergences between price and the TICK occur when price makes a higher high (or lower low) and the TICK makes a lower high (or higher low). These signals are often followed by short term reversals or corrections."

Bearish divergence: Price HH, TICK LH (or lower overbought high).
Bullish divergence: Price LL, TICK HL (or higher oversold low).

### Entry trigger
Bearish divergence (short entry) [06:30]:
"after a swing to the upside, the price again retested its intraday high at the upper end of the resistance zone, and the TICK exceeded +1000. However, this TICK overbought high was lower than the previous TICK overbought high. This divergence between price and the TICK meant the market was rallying with fewer stocks making upticks — a sign of internal weakness — and set up another short sale opportunity when price dropped back below the lower resistance level."

Bullish divergence (long entry) [07:00]:
"price makes a new low but the TICK indicator makes a higher low. In this example, price fell to a new low but the corresponding TICK low was higher than its previous intraday low. A long trade would have been entered when price traded back above the first low just, with a stop placed below the most recent low."

### Stop loss
[07:00-07:30] "with a stop placed below the most recent low" for longs; for shorts, implied above resistance.

### Context / bias filter
[08:00-08:30] Trend context required: "In a strong uptrend, any countertrend movement is usually marked by the TICK fluctuating between –100 and +100. In these situations,–100 becomes the oversold level. More often than not, the TICK tests the zero line."

Uptrend context: Entry on TICK zero-line crossover (oversold condition).
Downtrend context: Entry on TICK zero-line crossover (overbought condition).

### Entry trigger
Uptrend long entry [08:30-09:00]:
"In a strong uptrend, any countertrend movement is usually marked by the TICK fluctuating between –100 and +100...These tests offer opportunities to enter in the direction of the prevailing trend. In this example, SPY was in an uptrend and the TICK reached an overbought level above +500, which indicates a strong, uptrending market. In such a situation, the goal is to buy on a pullback."

Specific entry [09:00]:
"As the TICK dropped below zero, price also reached the 50 percent Fibonacci retracement line. The combination of the TICK oversold signal and the 50 percent retracement offered a higher probability for an up move."

Downtrend short entry [09:30]:
"In this example, we have a downtrend, the TICK moved toward the 0 level and the price stopped at this pivot line."

### Mechanizability
PARTIAL. TICK Index calculation is mechanizable (upticks minus downticks). However, all three strategies have significant gaps:
- Strategy 1: TICK extreme detection (+1000/-1000) is mechanizable; price key level identification is discretionary; entry on "price rejection" requires pattern detection
- Strategy 2: Divergence detection (price HH + TICK LH) is mechanizable; key level and trendline confirmation are discretionary
- Strategy 3: Zero-line crossover is mechanizable; Fibonacci/pivot level detection is mechanizable; but trend context ("strong uptrend") and pullback confirmation lack specificity

All strategies lack take profit rules. A basic skeleton can be coded with TICK extremes and key levels + price divergences, but complete mechanization requires defining: key level identification algorithm, rejection criteria, trend strength thresholds, and exit targets.

## Summary

The video teaches a 1-minute forex scalping strategy using three exponential moving averages (50, 100, 150-period) to identify strong trends and pullback opportunities. All three EMAs must agree in direction (bullish or bearish) at 45-degree angles (30-60 degrees acceptable) to confirm trend strength. Trades enter on pullbacks to the 50 or 100 EMA, confirmed by price closing back in trend direction above the 50 EMA. Exit tactics emphasize moving stops to breakeven as soon as possible (5-6 pips profit), with 0.5% risk-per-trade maximum. The strategy targets "trapped traders" for extra move leverage when price re-crosses the 50 EMA.

## Strategy: Three-EMA Pullback Scalp

### Context / bias filter (Trend Confirmation)

**Step 1: Identify Strong Trend**
"All three EMA's must be trending in agreement in a bullish or bearish manner" [03:30]

"Ideally, you want to see all three EMA's trending at a 45-degree angle, which identifies a strong trend. However, any slope ranging from 30- to 60-degrees will work just fine" [03:30-04:00]

"This is the image we'll search on our chart, meaning all three moving averages in agreement, in line, almost parallel, with strong trend consensus" [01:30-02:00]

Bullish signal: "price pushed above the three EMA's and traded steadily higher, the price staying above the 50-period EMA the entire time" [01:30]

**Trend Integrity Rule:** "Until the price breaks below the 150-period EMA, the bullish trend will remain intact" [02:30]

### Entry trigger (LONG in Uptrend)

**Step 2: Wait for Pullback**
"Any pull-back to the first or second EMA offers a buying (or selling) opportunity back in the direction of the established trend" [03:00]

"The second step involves waiting for the market to pull back to test either the 50- or 100-period EMA. Price can close beyond the 50-period EMA, but you generally do not want price to close beyond the 100-period EMA, or to be near the 150 EMA" [04:00]

**Step 3: Confirmation**
"The entry is triggered when price closes back in the direction of the current trend, beyond the 50-period EMA" [04:30]

"we look for price to close back below the 50-period average in order to trigger a long position" (in downtrend example) [06:30]

**Trade Logic:** "we'll search for a pullback trade, to pinpoint undervalued and overvalued entries into established trends" [02:30]

### Entry trigger (SHORT in Downtrend)

Inverse of long entry: "look for price to break above the 50-day EMA in order to set the stage for a 'sell' scenario. Once price dips above the first EMA, we look for price to close back below the 50-period average" [06:30]

### Stop loss

"set my loss stop below the low of the pull-back i am trading. Therefore, your stop loss would be beneath the low of the bar that tested the 50 or 100 period EMA" [07:00]

### Take profit / exit

"I move my stops to breakeven as soon as possible. You want to diminish your risk... when I'm scalping I look to play the breakeven trade when I'm 5-6 pips in profit" [05:00-05:30]

Alternative tactics: [05:30-06:00]

## Mechanizability

PARTIAL

EMA calculation (50, 100, 150-period) is mechanically computable. EMAs trending in agreement can be detected (all increasing or all decreasing). 45-degree angle (or 30-60 range) requires slope calculation via linear regression. Pullback to EMA and close back through EMA can be mechanized. However, significant gaps: (1) slope angle "agreement" measurement is subjective (30-60 degree range is vague); (2) "near" the 150 EMA lacks precision; (3) "trapped traders" explanation is narrative, not computable; (4) 5-6 pip breakeven entry timing is discretionary; (5) "strong trend" definition lacks objective threshold. The entry framework is mechanizable, but trend strength validation and exit timing require manual interpretation.

## Summary
This video explains how to properly combine technical indicators to avoid confusion and bad trade signals. It categorizes indicators into four classes (trend, momentum, volume, volatility), explains why combining indicators from the same class is redundant, and recommends using 2-3 indicators maximum from different classes. While example combinations are given (200 EMA + Stochastic, OBV + moving average, Bollinger Bands + RSI), no complete mechanizable strategy is presented.

## Indicator classes explained

### Trend-Following Indicators [00:30-01:00]
Designed to measure strength and direction of trend. Buy signal in uptrend, sell signal in downtrend. Lag the price and give signals after trend established. Examples: moving averages, Parabolic SAR, ADX, MACD [01:00]

### Momentum Indicators / Oscillators [01:30-02:00]
Measure relative strength of recent price moves, plot value 0-100. Overbought levels = sell signal, oversold levels = buy signal. Work well in ranging markets, cause whipsaws in trending markets. Examples: RSI, Stochastic, Momentum oscillator [02:00]

### Volume Indicators [02:00-02:30]
Measure strength of price move using trading volume. Popular for stock traders, unreliable in forex due to lack of volume measures. Examples: Chaikin Oscillator, OBV [02:30]

### Volatility Indicators [02:30-03:00]
Measure rate of price changes regardless of direction. Rise when markets fast, fall when slow. Examples: Bollinger Bands, ATR [03:00]

## Common indicator combination mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using multiple indicators from same class [04:00-05:00]
Example: using RSI, MACD, and Stochastic together (all momentum indicators) provides redundant information, all rise/fall together. Similarly, using Parabolic SAR, moving averages, and Keltner Channels (all trend indicators) show same information. Result: over-emphasizing information and missing other crucial clues.

### Mistake 2: Using too many indicators [06:00-07:30]
Pattern described [06:30-07:30]:
1. Find promising strategy on forum/YouTube
2. Backtest, see good entries but missing confirmation
3. Add more indicator filters
4. Backtest again, better results but still some losses
5. Add "filter" after "filter" until chart resembles "Christmas tree"
6. Indicators conflict with each other
7. By time all agree, price moved 50 pips and too late to enter

Result: "You started with a potentially good strategy and modified it almost completely" [07:30]

## Best practices for indicator combination [08:00-08:30]

- Limit use of indicators to 2 or 3 max (preferably 2) [08:00-08:30]
- Do not try to filter out all losing trades with indicators (will filter out good trades as well) [08:30]
- Don't add new indicator after a loss (losses occur even with 100 indicators) [08:30]
- Let indicators confirm trade bias, don't follow them blindly [08:30]

Core principle: "The best strategy with multiple indicators combine indicators that show a different type of information" [06:00]

## Recommended indicator combinations [08:30-10:30]

### Combination 1: Trend indicator + Momentum indicator [08:30-09:00]
Example: 200 EMA for trend + Stochastic for confirmation/entry [08:30-09:00]
- When price above 200 EMA: plan long trades based on Stochastic divergences [09:00]
- When price below 200 EMA: take short trades based on Stochastic divergences [09:00]

### Combination 2: Volume indicator + Trend indicator [09:00-09:30]
Example: OBV + moving average [09:30]
- Add long-term moving average on OBV to determine trend direction
- When OBV above moving average: search for long positions in direction of main trend [09:30]
- When OBV below moving average: take only short positions [09:30]

### Combination 3: Volatility indicator + Momentum indicator [09:30-10:00]
Example: Bollinger Bands + RSI [10:00]
- Use to search for divergences when Bollinger Bands are flat [10:00]

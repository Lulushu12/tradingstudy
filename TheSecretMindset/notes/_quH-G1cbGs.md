## Summary
An educational video on Renko charts—an alternative charting method that plots bricks based on price movement (not time) to filter market noise and reveal trends, support/resistance, and patterns more clearly. Covers Renko fundamentals, brick size setting (fixed amount vs. ATR-based), characteristic behaviors (time independence, price point gaps), practical uses (trend identification, pattern recognition, supply/demand zones), and integration with moving averages. Does not provide a complete trading strategy with entry, stop loss, or profit target rules.

### Renko Chart Fundamentals

**Definition and Purpose** [00:00-01:00]:
- Renko charts view price differently from time-based candlestick charts
- Chart looks like "a series of bricks placed at a 45-degree angle to each other" [00:30]
- "Filters out minor price movements" [00:00]
- "Price trends are easier to spot on the Renko chart" [00:30]
- Less subjective than candlestick charts [07:30]

**Time Independence** [01:00-01:30, 05:30-06:00]:
- "Does not depend on time"
- "Plots a new bar only when the market moves beyond a certain amount" [01:00]
- No set time limit for brick formation: "It could take 4 minutes, three hours, or eight days. It all depends on how volatile the pricing of the asset is and what brick size you set." [06:00]
- Depends only on price action, creating "a smoothed chart with less noise" [01:00-01:30]

**Brick Characteristics** [01:30-01:00]:
- Each brick is "a candlestick with no shadows" [01:00]
- Brick has only body, no wicks (unless wicks are added as optional indicator) [05:00-05:30]
- Color indicates direction: green for up, red for down
- Reversal rule: "For a Renko chart to reverse, meaning the brick to change its color, price action must reverse by at least two brick sizes" [01:30]

### Critical Parameter: Brick Size

**Definition** [01:30-02:00]:
- Brick size is the price range that the market must cover before a new brick is plotted [01:30]
- "The only key parameter for Renko charts" [01:30]
- Can be set in pips, ticks, or cents depending on market [01:30-02:00]

**Market-Specific Settings** [02:00-02:30]:
- **Forex (EUR/JPY)**: 5-pip brick size = 0.05 on Tradingview; 10-pip = 0.1 [02:00]
- **Forex (GBP/USD, EUR/USD)**: 10-pip brick size = 0.001 [02:00]
- **Stocks**: Box size of 1 = $1; "perfect for swing trading and even day trading strategies" [02:30]. Every $1 move creates new brick [02:30]

**Method 1: Fixed Amount** [02:30-04:00]:
- Use a set number of ticks/pips/cents as brick size [03:00]
- "Simplest method" but requires trial and error [03:00]
- Guidance [03:00-04:00]:
  - Reduce box size to see more bricks → scalping/day trading
  - Increase box size to see fewer bricks → swing trading
  - Too small = chart becomes too volatile and loses smoothing effect
  - Speaker's approach: "Eyeball the chart, and try a few different box sizes" [03:30-04:00]
  - "Smaller boxes mean more trend changes and more trades. Big boxes mean fewer trend changes and fewer trades." [04:00]

**Method 2: Average True Range (ATR)** [04:00-04:30]:
- Brick size based on 14-period ATR (typical value) [04:00-04:30]
- Advantage: "Factors in changes in price volatility" [04:00-04:30]
- Updates daily: "Should the ATR value change the next day, then this new ATR value will be used to set the brick size" [04:00-04:30]
- Speaker's preference: Fixed amount, because ATR changes "can cause the Renko chart to change significantly whenever it is updated" [04:30]

### Important Renko Characteristics

**Price Point Gaps** [04:30-05:30]:
- Renko charts don't show all price points [04:30-05:00]
- Example: If using 3-pip brick and price moves 5 pips opposite, 2 pips don't show [04:30-05:00]
- Optional: Indicators can add 'wicks' to show these gaps, but "start to add some of the 'noise' back to the chart" [05:00-05:30]
- "It's up to you if you want to add or remove the wicks on Renko bars" [05:00-05:30]

**No Time Axis** [05:30-06:00]:
- While a time axis appears at chart bottom, there's "no set time limit for how long a Renko box takes to form" [05:30-06:00]
- Entirely dependent on price volatility and brick size [06:00]

### Practical Uses of Renko Charts

**Trend Identification** [06:30-07:30]:
- "Identify uptrends by analyzing higher highs and higher lows" [07:00]
- "Downtrends with lower lows and lower highs" [07:00]
- "The stronger the trend, the more renko blocks will be plotted" [07:00]
- "Renko charts filter the noise by focusing exclusively on minimum price changes" [06:30]

**Support and Resistance** [07:00-07:30]:
- "Renko charts are particularly good at finding support and resistance areas because they condense long, but static periods" [07:00-07:30]
- "With classic candlestick charts, there is a lot of subjectivity and extra work that goes into identifying good and precise areas of support and resistance. In this example, notice how simple it is to plot the support and resistance levels." [07:30]
- Easier to "spot important highs and lows, and identify key support and resistance levels" [06:30]

**Chart Patterns** [08:00-08:30]:
- "Chart patterns are also much easier to recognize on a Renko chart" [08:00]
- Examples: "Inverted head and shoulders, a double top, flag patterns, triangles" [08:00-08:30]

**Supply and Demand Zones** [08:00-08:30]:
- "With the bricks on a Renko chart, you can spot price clusters quickly. These clusters are great demand and supply zones" [08:00-08:30]
- Identification: "Look for boxes formed by alternating bricks within a price range" [08:00-08:30]
- Example shown: "Several renko Bricks within the same price range, which projected a supply zone, which was respected by the market later on" [08:30]

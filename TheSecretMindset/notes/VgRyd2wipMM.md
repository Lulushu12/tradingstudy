Emphasizes that trading is "more about people than prices" and "game theory than economics."

## Controlled vs. Uncontrolled Elements (Educational Framework)

### Controlled elements discussed (no quantified rules)

- Analysis: "you control how thorough your analysis is" [06:00]
- Entry/Exit timing: "you get to make the decision for where and when to act" [06:30]
- Market and timeframe choice: "you decide when, where, how long" [06:30]
- Risk-reward ratio filtering [06:30]
- Position sizing: "you decide how much you risk on a position" [07:00]
- Economic calendar awareness: "schedule of these events are pre-planned and published ahead of time" [07:00-07:30]
- Psychological state training: "somewhat under your control" [07:30]
- Stop loss and target placement: "in realistic locations, relative to the market conditions" [08:00]

### Uncontrolled elements discussed

- Trigger conditions alignment: "you can never time when all the right conditions align" [08:00]
- Available information: retail traders lack information edge, no volume data for forex [08:30]
- Price volatility: "when price moves in a strong manner, you can only see it after it happens" [09:00]
- Unexpected news events: "can never know when they're about to erupt" [09:30]
- Take profit guarantee: "setting trade targets does not guarantee prices will reach it" [09:30]
- Life circumstances: "not always optimal to support your trading" [10:00-10:30]

### Overtrading warning signs (diagnostic, not quantified)

- Closing trades for loss but believing shouldn't have taken the trade [11:00]
- Looking at lower timeframes than planned and finding trades [11:30]
- Spending excessive time gazing at charts [11:30]
- Forcing trades by spending hours attempting to find one [11:30-12:00]

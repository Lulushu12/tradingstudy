## Elliott Wave Framework Components

### Core Elliott Wave Rules
**Wave 2 Rule:** "Wave 2 never pulls back more than 100% of wave 1. If it does, the trend cannot be confirmed" [01:00]

**Wave 3 Rule:** "Wave 3 cannot be the shortest of the 3 impulse waves. When we see a third wave that is too short, it means it is not a correct wave count" [01:00]

**Wave 4 Rule:** "Wave 4 cannot go below the price of Wave 1. If wave point 4 breaks below wave point 1, it clearly tells us that this is not part of the fourth wave" [01:00-01:30]

**General Enforcement:** "Each wave pattern has a distinct set of rules, which have to be fulfilled in order to make the pattern valid. If certain requirements are not met, some other pattern must be unfolding" [01:00]

### Wave Types: Impulse vs Corrective
**Impulse Waves:**
- Large price moves in the direction of the trend
- In uptrend: reach higher prices because upward moves > downward corrective moves [02:00]
- Provide better profit opportunities than corrective waves [02:30]
- Should be the focus for trading [02:00-02:30]

**Corrective Waves:**
- Smaller waves occurring within a trend
- Move against the predominant trend
- Should be used as entry points to catch next impulse wave [02:30]
- "Buy during pullbacks or corrective waves during uptrends, and ride the next impulse wave as it takes the price higher. Sell during corrective waves in a downtrend to profit from the next impulse wave down" [02:30]

**Trend Reversal Signal:**
- "If a market shows big moves upward with small corrective waves in between and then a much larger downward move occurs, this signifies the uptrend may be over" [03:00]
- "A big downward move larger than prior corrective waves and as large as the upward impulse waves indicates the trend is reversing" [03:00-03:30]

### Stop Loss Placement Based on Wave Positions
**Wave 3 Trading:**
- "Wave 2 cannot go below the low of Wave 1. This means that the best location to place a stop-loss order will be below the low of Wave 1" [04:00]
- Stop goes below Wave 1's low [04:00]

**Wave 5 Trading:**
- "Wave 4 cannot overlap with Wave 1. This means that when trading Wave 5, the best point to place a stop loss would be below the high of Wave 1" [04:00]
- Stop goes below Wave 1's high [04:00]

### Three Corrective Structures

**Zig-Zag Formation:**
- Steep price moves against the predominant trend [05:00-05:30]
- "Wave B is typically shortest in length compared to Waves A and C" [05:30]
- "These zig-zag patterns can happen twice or even three times in a correction" [05:30]

**Flat Formation:**
- Sideways corrective waves [05:30-06:00]
- "The lengths of the waves are GENERALLY equal in length, with wave B reversing the wave A move and wave C undoing the wave B move" [06:00]

**Triangle Formation:**
- Corrective patterns bounded by "either converging or diverging trend lines" [06:00]
- "Made up of 5-waves that move against the trend in a sideways fashion" [06:00]
- "Can be symmetrical, descending, ascending, or expanding" [06:00]
- Purpose: "Pre-trade analysis has to go into knowing what kind of corrective pattern is unfolding so that you know when it will be complete" [06:00-06:30]

### Divergence at Corrections
- "Corrections usually end in diagonals" [07:00]
- "Divergences can be found on momentum indicators" at correction end [07:00]
- Definition: "Divergence occurs when the price reaches a new extreme, while the line of the indicator does not" [06:30-07:00]
- Meaning: "Despite the new high or low, the current trend is exhausted and might be about to end soon" [07:00]
- Tools: "Oscillator indicators are the best tool to spot a divergence. I personally prefer to add the Stochastic or MACD histogram when I'm counting waves" [07:00]

### Wave Projection Methods

**Wave 3 Projection (Channel Method):**
1. Draw channel connecting origin of Wave 1 and end of Wave 2
2. Draw parallel line from top of Wave 1
3. This channel is "absolute minimum target for the 3rd wave"
4. If Wave 3 fails to reach upper line: trend is weak
5. Lower trend line serves as stop-loss
6. "Wave 3 is normally the strongest wave and often will go far beyond the upper trend line" [08:30-09:30]

**Wave 4 Projection:**
1. Draw channel connecting end of Wave 1 and Wave 3
2. Draw parallel line from end of Wave 2
3. Projects target for Wave 4
4. "Normally the baseline from wave 3 will be broken slightly by price action of wave 4"
5. "If wave 4 doesn't come near the baseline at all, this is a sign of strong trend" [09:30-10:30]

**Wave 5 Projection - Method 1:**
1. Connect end of Wave 2 and Wave 4 with trend line
2. Draw parallel line from end of Wave 3 projecting upward
3. This is target for Wave 5 (normal wave)
4. "An extended wave 5 would push higher"
5. "If the price fails to hit the projected trend line to wave 5, then the market is weak and you should look for a sell-off" [10:30-11:00]

**Wave 5 Projection - Method 2 (Vertical Rise Detection):**
1. If Wave 3 shows "nearly vertical rise or decline"
2. Draw trend line connecting Wave 2 and 4
3. Draw parallel line from Wave 1
4. "This parallel line will cut through wave 3 and will target wave 5"
5. Speaker states: "From my experience, this is a very valuable channel" [11:00-11:30]

### Mechanizability
PARTIAL
Elliott Wave counting has definable rules (Wave 2 ≤ 100% of Wave 1, Wave 3 not shortest, Wave 4 > Wave 1 low), and Fibonacci ratios are mechanical. However, critical gaps prevent full automation: (1) initial wave identification (where does wave 1 start?), (2) corrective structure type identification (zig-zag vs flat vs triangle) relies on visual assessment, (3) "the easiest point from which you start a wave count" is subjective [07:00], (4) determining which waves to count (diagonal vs standard) is discretionary, (5) channel trend line drawing positions are visually approximate. The framework is interpretive and requires significant human judgment.

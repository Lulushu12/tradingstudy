## Strategy 6: Contrarian Fade on Failed News Reactions

### Indicators and settings

- Trigger: Economic release, corporate earnings, or technical breakout [13:00]
- Reaction type: Bullish news but price cannot hold the move [12:30]

### Context / bias filter

When a market "had every reason to continue in that direction and still couldn't" [13:30]. "If the market couldn't hold a bullish push on good news, it implies hidden selling or a lack of real buying interest" [13:00].

### Entry trigger

Watch for "failed reaction" to news [12:30]. Example: "NFP news... numbers are released and they look bullish. You expect price to rise and price does spike up, but then it stalls and sinks back below the starting point" [12:30]. "If that move can't stick, they get trapped. Meanwhile, contrarians see the failure as a sign the market wants to go the other way" [13:30]. Entry: "So, you fade the move. You do the opposite of what a naive approach might suggest" [13:00]. Short entry example: "stock gaps up at the open but can't hold... Within minutes, it's back below the pre-gap level. Contrarians might short" [13:30].

Entry is also confirmed by: "If you see that price is returning below a daily pivot or a missed pivot after good news, that's an added sign the bounce is failing" [14:00].

### Stop loss

"If you're shorting, you can place a stop just above that day's high or the initial spike. If the market retakes that high, maybe the fade was early" [14:30].

### Take profit / exit

"If it continues lower, you might catch a good move" [14:30]. Can refine with volume: "If you see huge volume on the reversal bar, it might confirm big money selling" [15:30].

### Invalidation / skip conditions

## Summary

This video presents ten best practices for using Fibonacci retracements in trading rather than a single mechanizable strategy. The key principles are: only use Fibonacci in clear trends (not ranges), apply it to larger timeframes, identify correct swing highs/lows, and layer in additional confirmation (moving averages, volume, divergences, candlestick patterns). One specific strategy is presented: the Golden Ratio Retreat Strategy, which enters on price rejection at the 61.8 Fibonacci level and scales out on subsequent Fib levels.

## General Principles (Non-Strategic)

### Principle 1: Use Fibonacci Only in Trends [00:00-00:30]
"Fibs do not work well in consolidations, corrections, ranges and sideways moves" [00:30]. "There must be a visible trend otherwise FIB levels won't have a meaningful impact on price movement" [00:00].

### Principle 2: Use Larger Timeframes [01:00-02:00]
"Applying Fibonacci retracements over a short timeframe is ineffective. The shorter the timeframe, the less reliable the retracement levels" [01:00-01:30]. "Try to focus on larger time frames when you apply FIB levels" [01:30-02:00].

### Principle 3: Apply to Correct Price Swings [02:30-03:30]
"You determine the dominant trend, you identify the swing high and swing low before drawing the Fibonacci lines" [02:30-03:00]. "In a bullish trend, draw the Fibonacci from the swing low to the swing high. In a bearish trend, draw the Fibonacci from the swing high to the swing low" [03:00]. "You will be drawing the Fibonacci tool from the wicks of the swing highs and lows" [03:00]. "The clearer the swing low, the clearer the swing high and the clearer the trend between these 2 points, the more accurate a Fibonacci Retracement will be" [03:00-03:30].

### Principle 4: Shallow vs Deep Retracements [04:00-05:00]

**Shallow Retracements [04:30]:**
In strong, clearly established trends, look for shallow pullbacks to 23.6%, 38.2%, and 50% Fib levels before the trend continues.

**Deep Retracements [04:30-05:00]:**
When a trend is not yet clearly established, price can test 61.8%, 78.6%, and 88.6% Fib levels multiple times without breaking them (which would invalidate the trend).

**Trend Failure [05:00]:**
"If the price retraces 100% of the last price wave, that may mean the trend has failed" [05:00].

## Strategy 1: The Golden Ratio Retreat Strategy

### Indicators and settings

- Fibonacci retracements: levels 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% (Golden Ratio), 78.6%, 88.6%, 100% [05:30-06:30]
- No other indicators required for the core setup

### Context / bias filter

A downtrend with Fibonacci levels drawn from swing high to swing low [05:30].

### Entry trigger

"If the price moves up above the 50% retracement, but does not close above the 61.8 Golden Ratio, look for a bearish entry" [05:30-06:00]. "It is OK for the wick of the candle to break the 61.8 line as long as the price closes below it" [06:00].

### Stop loss

"If the price never gets higher than the 61.8 then you can set your initial stop just above that line" [06:00-06:30]. "If the price went higher than the 61.8 then you could set a stop above the high of the candle that broke the level" [06:30].

### Take profit / exit

Scaling exit strategy [06:00-06:30]:

1. "As the price bounces from the 61.8 Golden Ratio, look for a possible reversal move back down to the 38.2 level" [06:00]
2. "If the price breaks the 38.2 level, the 23.6 may be its next hesitation point" [06:00]
3. "If that doesn't hold, expect a possible 100% retracement back to the 0 line" [06:00]
4. "These levels are good potential places to consider scaling out of the trade" [06:00-06:30]
5. "If scaling out of the trade, consider moving the stops to the next Fib line as the targets are met" [06:30]

### Invalidation / skip conditions

- Entry invalidated if price closes above 61.8% level (though wick break is allowed) [06:00]
- Setup not valid in non-trending environments

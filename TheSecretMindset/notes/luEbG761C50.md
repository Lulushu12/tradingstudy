## Strategy 1: Prior Day High/Low Breakouts

### Logic and settings

Monitor previous day's high and low as magnet levels. Watch for:
- Price breaks through the level [00:30]
- Price bounces off the level [00:30]
- Price rejects the level [00:30]

### Entry concept

**Bullish breakout:** Clear break above prior day's high with volume rise signals bigger gains; enter on break [01:30]

**Bearish breakdown:** Price pushes below prior day's low but struggles to go lower, may climb back [00:30]

**Failed move:** If break stalls or fails, close out fast, expecting slide back into day's range [00:30]

**Retest confirmation:** Wait for retest of these highs or lows to confirm support or resistance [01:30]

### Performance indicators

- Strong surge on retest = bigger participants stepping in [01:30]
- Weak spike on retest = break might not hold [01:30]
- Volume failure at break = weakness, hint to fade move expecting drift back [01:00]

## Strategy 2: Footprint Charts and Bid/Ask Imbalances

### Logic and settings

Footprint charts split each candlestick into two columns: bid volume and ask volume [02:30]. Each block represents executed contracts/shares at each price level [03:00].

**Imbalance detection:** Occurs when one side greatly exceeds other at given price level [03:00]. Large difference between bid and ask volume indicates which side is more aggressive [03:30].

### Entry concept

**Bullish imbalance:** Repeated buy imbalances in fast-moving uptrend reinforce bullish pressure [03:30]

**Bearish imbalance:** Repeated sell imbalances suggest big participants forcing price lower [03:30]

**Absorption detection:** One side absorbing other side's orders. In uptrend, hidden pockets of supply (large selling imbalances) might stop the move [04:00]. If buy side absorbs sellers, price breaks higher; if sellers hold the line, price turns [04:30].

### Vagueness log

1. "Repeated" imbalances - UNDEFINED-PARAM: how many repetitions?
2. "Big participants" - SUBJECTIVE: how to identify size from volume data alone?
3. "Absorption" - SUBJECTIVE: visually assessed, not computable threshold
4. "Hidden pockets of supply" - UNDEFINED-RULE: detected visually, not with formula

### Mechanizability

PARTIAL - Bid/ask volume tracking is mechanical. However, identifying significant imbalances, interpreting absorption, and distinguishing "big participants" from normal flow requires subjective judgment.

## Strategy 3: Hidden Pivot Clusters

### Logic and settings

Clusters occur when three or four pivot highs or lows stack near one price [05:00]. Pivots stack in same neighborhood [05:30].

**Cluster characteristics:**
- Not exact single lines; are areas on chart [06:00]
- May coincide with daily pivot formula calculations, strengthening zone [06:00]
- Can be hidden inside candle wicks; multiple wicks poking into same area [06:30]
- Tight clusters: pivots very close together, can create sharp reversals [07:00]
- Wide clusters: pivots spread over big range, lead to choppier action [07:00]

### Entry concept

Price might react at cluster (pause, bounce off, or break through) [05:00-05:30]. Clusters treated as wide support/resistance zones [06:00]. Clusters often become magnets for orders [07:30].

## Strategy 4: TPO Charts (Time Price Opportunity)

### Logic and settings

TPO charts display time at price in letter-based format [08:00]. Each time frame broken into letters; each letter aligns with price level in that time frame [08:00]. Reveals how much time spent at each price (NOT obscured by candle wicks) [08:30].

### Entry concept

**Balanced session:** Big cluster around middle of day [08:30]

**Bullish acceptance:** Cluster pushed up near top shows participants welcomed higher pricing [08:30]

**Bearish acceptance:** Cluster near bottom shows persistent acceptance at lower levels [08:30]

**Quick rejection:** Short stack of letters at certain price shows market tested but quickly moved away; swift rejection [09:00]. Single or two letters can mark strong rejection at extremes [09:30].

**Point of control:** Middle area often becomes POC - price level with most TPOs [10:00]

**Value area:** Price range where 70% of day's TPO activity took place [10:30]. Presumably the day's fair zone [10:30].

**Day open signal:**
- Opens above prior day's value area and builds letters at higher levels = bullish acceptance [11:00]
- Drops back into previous day's value area = failure to sustain new ground [11:00]

### Vagueness log

1. "Big cluster" - SUBJECTIVE: what constitutes "big"?
2. "Balanced session" - SUBJECTIVE: no clear threshold for balance
3. "Strong rejection" - SUBJECTIVE: single letter is not precisely defined
4. "70% of activity" - This is stated but NOT how to implement it

### Mechanizability

FULL - TPO analysis is mechanical once data is available. Point of control and value area calculation are computable (mode of prices by time, 70% confidence interval).

## Strategy 5: Trend Line + Volume Spike Tests

### Logic and settings

Draw steady line through points on chart [11:30]. Watch how volume changes each time price touches the line [11:30].

### Entry concept

**Line holds (rising trend line example):**
- Volume expands at contact point but price does not break lower = buyers holding line [11:30]
- Price closes firm above line = buyers outweigh sellers, uptrend may continue [12:00]

**Line breaks:**
- Volume spikes at trend line as price breaks = line is weaker barrier [12:00]

**Position building:** Sideways drifting near line with climbing volume bars might suggest big players building positions; price may explode up or down [13:00-13:30]

**Breakout quality:** Strong breakout with wide range bar plus large activity confirms path [13:30]. Breakout with low volume will often fade [13:30].

## Strategy 6: Range Analysis (Bar Expansion/Contraction)

### Logic and settings

Examine each bar's range (high minus low) [14:00]. Reflects market energy [14:00].
- High = how far buyers push
- Low = how far sellers push
- Range = difference; shows if market slow or alive [14:00]

**Big range:** Traders pushing/pulling with greater force [14:00]
**Small range:** Caution or waiting [14:00]

### Entry concept

**Range expansion:** Large bar means many players jumped in; volatility surge, catch some off guard [15:00]. Some believe market is on move; others step aside [15:00].

**Contraction:** Smaller bars show traders waiting; no clear path, highs not too high, lows not too low [15:30]. Creates cluster of narrow bars. Some traders anticipate breakout from quiet stages [15:30].

**First big bar from contraction:** Can bring energy, hooking new orders into market [16:00]. Others remain cautious [16:00].

## Strategy 7: Candle Close Location Analysis

### Logic and settings

Where each bar closes relative to its high-low range [16:30]:
- Close near high = buyers kept pushing until end [16:30]
- Close near low = larger selling pressure toward bar's finish [16:30]
- Close around midpoint = balance between buyers and sellers [17:00]

**Quantification method:** Assign value based on close location [18:30]:
- Close near high = 1.0
- Close near low = 0
- Close in middle = 0.5
- Calculate average over set of bars [18:30]

### Entry concept

**Bullish momentum:**
- Many bars close near highs = buyers consistently winning [17:00]
- In uptrend, streak of bars finishing high = strong interest at each step [17:00]
- Average close location stays near 0.8 = steady bullish pressure [18:30]

**Reversal signals:**
- Bar suddenly closes near low after high-close streak = shift in mood [17:00]
- Low close coincides with wider ranges or bigger candles = sudden wave of selling [17:30]
- Market moving upward but candles closing near midpoints with shadows on top = sellers active even if price climbing; foreshadow stall [18:00]

**Indecision:** Average oscillates around 0.5 = market looks indecisive [18:30]

**Shift in tone:** Swift drop toward 0.1 = big change in tone [19:00]

**Downtrend reversal:** Market in downtrend beginning to close near high of each bar = stronger buying than before [18:30]

### Vagueness log

1. "Near high," "near low" - SUBJECTIVE: no specific percentile threshold for "near" (e.g., top 25%, top 10%?)
2. "Steady" vs "indecisive" - SUBJECTIVE: no specific thresholds for 0.8, 0.5 values

### Mechanizability

PARTIAL - Close location calculation is mechanical. However, defining what "near" means (which percentile of range) is subjective.

## Strategy 8: Measured Moves

### Logic and settings

Market rises certain distance, pulls back, drives higher again. Distance from first surge helps estimate how far next wave might go [19:30].

Take size of first move, project it above pullback [19:30].

### Entry concept

Watch if price hits projected target [19:30]. If meets target and stalls = confirms measured move idea [19:30]. If blasts past target = reveals extra strength [19:30].

**Example [20:00]:**
- Clean uptrend: price rallies 100 pips from swing low
- Retraces about 50 pips (forms short pause)
- Measures first wave and plots same distance (100 pips) from swing low of pullback
- That new level becomes reference

**Symmetrical moves [20:30]:** Series of waves mirroring each other in size confirms trend has steady rhythm [20:30]. Multiple measured moves that keep playing out give sense trend is healthy and consistent [20:30].

**Caveat [21:00]:** Markets don't always honor moves precisely; may come close then fade or power through [21:00]. Offers ballpark estimate, not absolute rule [21:00].

**Overshoot/undershoot interpretation [21:30]:**
- Overshoots = strong momentum [21:30]
- Undershoots = weaker momentum than before [21:30]

### Vagueness log

1. "Certain distance" - NOT STATED: which prior move is referenced if multiple candidates exist?
2. "Same distance" - UNDEFINED-RULE: which pullback to measure from? percentage retracement?
3. "Stalls" - SUBJECTIVE: no specific price or time criteria

### Mechanizability

PARTIAL - If pullback selection is defined, measured move projection is mechanical. However, swing selection and pullback identification require subjective judgment.

## Summary

The video introduces supply and demand zone trading as a framework for identifying where banks accumulate positions. Demand zones form at the origin of sharp bullish moves (marked with rectangles from the pre-move low/candle). Supply zones form at the origin of sharp bearish moves (marked from the pre-move high/candle). The strategy assumes banks must split large positions into smaller chunks and later return to fill the remainder, creating the retest. The video emphasizes that zones are ONE-TIME-USE; once price reverses in a zone, it loses power. Recent zones outperform old zones. Zones formed from consolidation bases are stronger than single-candle zones due to trapped trader liquidity.

## Core Concepts

### Supply vs. Demand

- **Demand zones:** "represent points where the banks have placed a significant number of buy positions. These are the support levels" [02:30]
  - Form at the source of sharp bullish moves (single candle or base) [03:30-04:00]
  - Marked from the prior bearish candle low up to the most recent low before the move [06:30-07:00]

- **Supply zones:** "points where the banks place a significant number of sell positions and these are resistance points where price could fall" [02:30]
  - Form at the source of sharp bearish moves (single candle or base) [03:30]
  - Marked from the prior bullish candle open up to the most recent high before the drop [05:30-06:00]

### Why Banks Create Supply/Demand Zones

"Banks must break their positions into smaller chunks and place each trade individually, around a similar price... Their positions are so big that not enough people exist on the opposite side... they must let price move away and then make it return later on to get the rest of their position entered" [01:30-02:00]

"supply and demand trading is all about finding points where the banks have bought or sold and then jumping into trades when price returns to pick up the rest of the banks' position" [02:00]

## Zone Types and Formation

### Single Candle Zone

- **Supply:** Swift drop away from a single bullish candle [03:30]
- **Demand:** Large move up from a single bearish candle [04:00]
- Strength: Less trapped traders; less reliable [04:30-05:00]

### Base Zone (Consolidation-Based)

- Prior consolidation (few candles in tight range) before the sharp move [03:30]
- **Supply:** Base followed by sharp drop [03:30]
- **Demand:** Base followed by sharp rise [04:00]
- Strength: More trapped traders from consolidation; MORE RELIABLE [04:30-05:00]

Important: "Supply or demand zones which are formed from a base tend to result in more successful trades than zones which form from a single candle" [04:30]

## Drawing Supply and Demand Zones

### Drawing Supply Zones (from bearish move)

1. Identify the last BULLISH candle before the drop
2. Draw rectangle from the OPEN of that bullish candle
3. Drag up to the most recent high before the drop
4. This creates the supply zone rectangle [05:30-06:00]

Rule: "You always draw supply zones from the last BULLISH candle before the drop, if the candle immediately before the drop is bearish, then you need to locate the most recent bullish candle" [05:30]

### Drawing Demand Zones (from bullish move)

1. Identify the most recent BEARISH candle found before the up move
2. Draw rectangle from the OPEN of that bearish candle
3. Drag down to the most recent low [06:30-07:00]

Rule: "the candle you're drawing the demand zone off must be bearish. If it happens to be bullish you need to go back and locate the nearest bearish candle before the move up" [07:00]

Reasoning: "Banks do not place trades onto movements which are in the direction of the trade they are placing. If the current candle is bullish the banks wont be placing buy trades, if its bearish, they wont be placing sell trades" [07:30]

## Two Critical Rules

### Rule 1: Recent Zones Outperform Old Zones

"Old zones rarely work... the longer the market has been away from a supply or demand zone the better chance the market has of turning when it eventually returns. This is completely wrong. Recent zones are far more important" [08:00]

"trading zones which have been created recently are far more effective than trading zones which were created a long time ago" [08:00]

### Rule 2: Zones Are ONE-TIME-USE

"Supply and demand zones are ONE TIME USE. Not two times or three times, one time ONLY. When price hits a zone and reverses, that's it; the zone loses its power" [08:30]

Exception: "The only exception to this rule is if a zone forms at the top or bottom of a consolidation. In this case, price will probably reverse two or three times from the zone, since the consolidation shows the banks are buying/selling from similar points. However, once the consolidation is over, the zone loses all its power" [08:30-09:00]

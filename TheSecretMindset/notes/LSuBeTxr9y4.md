## Summary

The video introduces Elliott Wave Theory as a framework for identifying recurring fractal price patterns. Prices move in impulsive waves (large moves in the trend direction) and corrective waves (smaller pullbacks). A complete 5-wave impulse is followed by a 3-wave correction (A-B-C). The strategy is to buy during corrective waves in uptrends and sell during corrective waves in downtrends, catching the next impulse move. Three immutable rules constrain wave counting; three guidelines offer probabilistic relationships. However, the speaker emphasizes that Elliott Wave is a "concept" not a precise trading technique, with subjective application being its main weakness.

## Core Concepts

### Impulse vs. Corrective Waves

- Impulse wave: "large price move" in the trend direction; "moves up are larger than the moves down" [01:00]
- Corrective waves: "smaller waves that occur within a trend" [01:30]
- Entry strategy: "buy during pullbacks or corrective waves during uptrends, and ride the next impulse wave... sell during corrective waves in a downtrend to profit from the next impulse wave down" [01:30-02:00]

### Five-Wave / Three-Wave Structures

Uptrend pattern: "five-wave pattern: impulse, correction, impulse, correction, and another impulse" (waves 1-5) [03:00]
followed by "three waves lower: an impulse down, a correction to the upside, and then another impulse down" (waves A-B-C) [03:00-03:30]

Fractality: "these movements are fractal, meaning the pattern occurs on small and large time frames. For example, the first impulse wave higher within an uptrend on a daily chart is composed of five waves on an hourly chart" [03:30]

### Typical Correction Sizes

Wave 2: "typically 60 percent the length of wave one. If wave one advances $1, then wave two will likely see the price drop by about 60 cents" [05:00-05:30]

Wave 4: "typically 30 to 40 percent the size of wave three. For example, if wave three rallied $2, the price is likely to drop 60 to 80 cents during wave four" [05:30-06:00]

Wave 3: "often the largest, usually much bigger than wave one" [05:30]

Caveat: "These are averages seen over many trades and trends. Corrections may be smaller or larger than average on any single trade" [06:00]

## Three Golden Rules of Elliott Wave

### Rule #1: Wave 3 Cannot Be Shortest

"Wave 3 can NEVER be the shortest impulse wave. So wave 3 should be the longest of the impulse waves but it cannot be the shortest which means that waves 1 or 5 CANNOT BE longer than wave 3" [06:30]

"the high of wave 3 must be higher than that of wave 1 and if it's not higher, you have to start your re-count" [06:30]

Additional: "if price does not exceed the high of wave 2, then that means there's no progress therefore you have to start the re-count" [06:30]

### Rule #2: Wave 2 Cannot Retrace Beyond Wave 1 Start

"Wave 2 can NEVER go beyond the start of Wave 1. In other words, Wave 2 shall not retrace more than 100 % of wave 1. If a break occurs below this low, you need to start your re-count" [07:00]

### Rule #3: Wave 4 Cannot Cross Wave 1 Territory

"Wave 4 can NEVER cross in the same price area as Wave 1, meaning that Wave 4 can never overlap wave 1. So the LOW OF WAVE 4 cannot go BELOW THE HIGH OF WAVE 1. If that happens, you need a re-count" [07:00-07:30]

## Three Elliott Wave Guidelines (Probabilistic)

### Guideline #1: Wave 5 = Wave 1 When Wave 3 is Longest

"when the wave 3 is the longer impulse wave, wave 5 will be almost/approximately equal to wave 1" [07:30-08:00]

### Guideline #2: Wave 2 and Wave 4 Correction Alternation

"the structure for wave 2 and wave 4 will alternate…if wave 2 is a sharp correction, wave 4 will be a flat correction. If wave 2 is flat, wave 4 will be sharp" [08:00]

### Guideline #3: ABC Correction Target

"after a 5 wave impulse advance, ABC corrections usually end in the area of prior wave 4 low" [08:00]

## Trading Strategy Simplification (Recommended for Beginners)

The speaker advises starting with core concepts only, not advanced wave labeling:

1. "only take trades in the direction of the impulse waves and take trades during the corrective waves" [08:30]
2. "Look for trade entry signals once the price has corrected the average amount" [08:30]
3. "The correction isn't likely to stop exactly at the percentage levels discussed before, so taking trades slightly above or below the described percentage levels is fine" [09:00]
4. "consider keeping track of each wave in the overall price structure" [09:00]
5. "Watching the direction of the impulse waves will signal potential trend changes" [09:00-09:30]

## Mechanizability

DISCRETIONARY

The video explicitly states: "Elliott Wave is not a trading technique. There are no specific rules of entry or exit, nor is there one 'right' way to use it in trading" [09:30-10:00]

The three golden rules can be verified computationally once a wave count is established. However, identifying which candles form which waves is purely subjective and manual. Correction percentage ranges are average-based, not fixed thresholds. The speaker acknowledges: "the theory can be complex to apply, as it isn't always easy isolating the five wave and three wave patterns... the apparently subjective nature of how it may be applied" [09:30-10:00]. Cannot be reliably automated without inventing specific wave identification rules.

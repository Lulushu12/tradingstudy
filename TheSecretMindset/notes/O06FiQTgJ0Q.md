A framework combining supply/demand zone identification with technical indicator confirmation (MACD divergence and modified stochastic) using a multiple timeframe approach. ## Supply/Demand Zone Identification (4 Methods)

### Method 1: Obvious Trend Direction Change [01:30 - 02:00]

**Supply zone**: 
- First point: the exact price open of the last bullish candle before the quick drop
- Second point: the highest candle wick of the range
- Look for large candle body or gap away from zone as indicator of strong price action [02:00]

**Demand zone**:
- First point: the open price of the last bearish candle before the price increase
- Second point: the lowest candle wick of the range
- Look for large candle body or gap away from zone [02:00]

### Method 2: Price Trading Sideways Then Shoots Away [02:00 - 02:30]

"Price trading in a narrow range for several bars and then drops dramatically" [02:00 - 02:30]. The narrow range becomes the zone. "Next time price came into the zone, the trend reversed giving us a short opportunity" [02:00 - 02:30].

### Method 3: Indecision/Corrections During Strong Trends [02:30 - 03:00]

- For supply zone: "ranging periods or small corrections during a strong downtrend"
- For demand zone: "ranging periods or small corrections during a strong uptrend"
- Pattern name: "rally base rally" and "drop base drop structures" [02:30 - 03:00]

### Method 4: Gaps [02:30 - 03:00]

"When a stock gaps either up or down, there has been a sudden change in the balance of supply and demand. The price it gaped from is very likely to be a strong supply or demand zone" [03:00].

## Zone Retest and Probability Framework

### First retest [04:00]:
"Very important, the first retrace back to the value area is the highest probability of a trade working out" [04:00].

### Second/third retest [04:00 - 04:30]:
"On the second or the third retest, the probability of the trade working out decreases" [04:00 - 04:30].

### Zone invalidation [04:30]:
"A supply and demand level becomes invalid once price action breaks through and closes above or below the tested level. This is because all orders that were left at that level have been absorbed" [04:30].

### Stale zones [04:30 - 05:00]:
"What happens when a value area is not fresh and price has been back to it one or more times? Chances are price will trade through the value area because all of the previous orders that were placed there have been filled or absorbed" [04:30 - 05:00].

## Strategy 2: Stochastic Confirmation Entry

### Indicators and settings
- Stochastic: Modified parameters
  - Standard overbought/oversold levels: 20/80 (default)
  - Modified levels: 10/90 (increased threshold) [11:00]
  - Length: 15, 5, and 3 (instead of typical 14, 3, 3) [11:00]
- Rationale: "This way, the indicator won't reach the levels as often" [11:00]

### Context / bias filter

Supply/demand zone on higher timeframe (4-hour or daily). Multiple timeframe approach: "We'll trade in the direction of the higher timeframe, especially for a trade like this that would be considered against the previous downtrend leading up to it" [11:30].

"Although I don't rely much on overbought and oversold conditions, these signals tend to work in combination with supply and demand zones" [11:00]. But: "Trading with these levels without price action is a guaranteed way to lose money trading" [11:00].

### Entry trigger

"Overbought and oversold" stochastic signal at supply/demand zone [11:00]. 

Example: "We see a nice up move, which followed this oversold stochastic signal" [11:30]. Alternative: "You could also wait for this trendline breakout if you really want to be sure that the bulls are taking full control" [11:30].

Second example: "On the 30-minute time frame, on the first retest of the supply zone, stochastic was well over 90, and price found new sellers in this area" [12:00].

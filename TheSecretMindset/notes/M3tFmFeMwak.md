## Summary
A price action strategy using four candle elements to read market sentiment without indicators: dominance (body = who is holding territory), failure (wicks = where the other side got trapped), effort (size = conviction behind the move), and location (context = where candle formed relative to key levels). The four-question framework provides a trade bias (bullish or bearish) based on candle structure at support/resistance levels. Designed for higher timeframes only, not scalping.

## Strategy 1: Four-Element Candle Analysis for Trade Bias

### Indicators and settings
No indicators used. Pure price action reading of candle structure: body, wicks, size, and position relative to key levels.

### Context / bias filter
**Location Requirement** [04:30-06:30]: "Where did that candle form? Same candle at resistance means something completely different than the same candle at support." [04:30]. Candles must form at key levels: "Wait for candles at your levels. Ignore everything else. Support, resistance, supply, demand. Those are your levels. When a candle forms there, read it. When a candle forms nowhere, ignore it." [13:00]. Candles in the middle of range have "low value information" [05:30-06:00].

**Dominance Analysis** [01:30-02:30]:
- Full body with tiny wicks = knockout (one side dominated completely)
- Tiny body with tiny wicks = draw (nobody won convincingly)
- Green body = buyers own this price range, took ground and kept it
- Red body = sellers own it, pushed down and held
- Tiny body = nobody owns anything yet, battle not over

**Failure Analysis (Wicks)** [02:30-03:30]: "Wicks are scars. Every wick tells you someone tried and got destroyed." [02:30]. 
- Long upper wick = buyers got trapped at that price (bought there, now underwater)
- Long lower wick = sellers got destroyed, buyers defended and pushed them back
- Longer wick = more violent failure, more real money trapped
- No wicks = clean dominance (no rejection, no trapped traders)

**Effort Analysis (Size)** [03:30-04:30]: "Effort doesn't lie. You just learn to decode, not just define." [04:30]
- Big candle = high participation, strong effort, institutions involved, real commitment
- Small candle = weak effort, low participation, retail pushing, probably a trap
- Size compared to recent bars reveals conviction
- Same direction/color, different size = different truth

**Context Analysis** [06:00-06:30]:
- Big red candle in downtrend = continuation (sellers pushing lower)
- Big red candle at major support = potentially exhaustion (sellers running out of steam)
- Same candle, different location, different story
- Big candle after 8-10 green candles = potentially exhaustion, move might be ending

**Bullish Candle at Resistance** [05:30-06:00]: 
- Small with wicks = rejection starting
- Big and clean = breakout potential

**Bearish Candle at Support** [05:30-06:00]:
- Small with wicks = defense holding
- Big and clean = breakdown potential

### Entry trigger
**Four-Question Framework** [06:30-07:30]: "Question one, who won? Look at the body. Green means buyers. Red means sellers. Question two, who failed? Look at the wicks. Long upper means buyers rejected. Long lower means sellers rejected... Question three, how convincing? Look at the size. Big means high conviction. Small means low conviction. Compare to recent bars. Question four, where? Look at the position. At a key level means high value read. At nothing means low value read." [06:30-07:00]

**One-Sentence Complete Read** [07:00-07:30]: "Use this format. Buyers won with high conviction after sellers tried and failed at support. That one sentence tells you who's winning, who failed, how committed, what's likely next." [07:00-07:30]

**Trade Bias Examples** [08:00-12:00]:
1. Small body + massive upper wick at resistance [08:00-08:30]: "Sellers defended this level violently. Buyers have no power here. Trade bias is short." [08:00-08:30]
2. Big green body + no wicks, breaking resistance [08:30-09:30]: "Buyers dominated... This breakout is probably real." [08:30-09:30]
3. Small red body + long lower wick at support [09:30-10:30]: "Sellers attacked support and got rejected. Support probably holds." [09:30-10:30]
4. Big green body after 8 green candles at resistance [10:30-11:30]: "Buyers pushed hard, but they're running on fumes at a dangerous level. This looks like a trap." [10:30-11:30]
5. Tiny body + long wicks both sides [11:30-12:00]: "Pure indecision... I sit on my hands. No trade until one side shows real commitment." [11:30-12:00]

**Rule 1: No Random Candles** [12:00-12:30]: "One candle in the middle of nowhere means nothing. It needs a level. It needs context. Don't read candles at random prices." [12:00-12:30]

**Rule 2: Wrong Timeframe** [12:30-13:00]: "One candle on the one minute chart is noise. One candle on the four hour chart is signal. Higher time frame candles carry more weight. You will be tempted to try this strategy for scalping. Don't. Use it on higher time frames only." [12:30-13:00]

**Rule 4: Skip Indecision** [11:30-12:00]: When a candle shows both upper and lower wicks with tiny body (both sides fighting), "sit on my hands. No trade until one side shows real commitment." [12:00]

### Mechanizability
PARTIAL. Candle body color (green/red) is objective. Wick presence and relative length can be measured (lower wick X% of candle range, upper wick Y% of candle range). Candle size relative to recent candles is computable once lookback period is defined. However, key levels (support, resistance, supply, demand) are identified manually and subjectively. The four-question framework produces a direction bias (short or long) but does not produce an explicit entry price, stop loss, or take profit. The framework teaches "reading" a candle to generate bias, not to execute a trade. Overall, 50% of the strategy is mechanically clear (candle structure analysis), while 50% is subjective (level identification, entry point, stop/profit targets).

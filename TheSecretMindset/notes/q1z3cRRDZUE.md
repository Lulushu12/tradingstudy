## Summary
A framework for identifying and trading sideways (consolidation) markets, which comprise "most markets" and require different approaches than trending markets. The methodology involves identifying the range bounds (support and resistance), mapping all internal support/resistance levels, identifying the midrange (50% Fibonacci level between high and low), and then implementing specific strategies: bounce trading at the edges, fading failed breakouts back into the range, or trading the midrange bounce. Successful range trading requires balancing conservative and aggressive stop placement to avoid whipsaws while capturing profits.

## Strategy 1: Bounce Trading at Range Support/Resistance

### Indicators and settings
- Range boundaries: upper and lower support/resistance levels [01:30]
- Identified by: side-to-side support and resistance levels or angular trend lines and channels [01:30]
- Internal levels: all minor and major support/resistance levels within the range [03:00]
- Midrange: 50% Fibonacci level between range high and range low [04:00]

### Context / bias filter
Market must be in consolidation/sideways phase, not trending [00:00]. Sideways markets characterized by [00:00]:
- Price not rolling in one definitive direction (up or down)
- Run side to side through short bursts
- Price reverses shortly after brief strong moves
- Reflects major indecision

Range must be wide enough to trade profitably [02:00]; very narrow ranges not worth trading [02:00].

### Entry trigger
Search for price action patterns at upper and lower levels of range, trading the bounce from those levels [08:00]. 

**Long entries (at range low):**
1. Price approaches lower range support [04:30]
2. Price bounces from this level [04:00]
3. Enter long at this bounce

**Short entries (at range high):**
1. Price approaches upper range resistance [04:30]
2. Price bounces from this level [04:00]
3. Enter short at this bounce

### Stop loss
Conservative: placed just below (for longs) or above (for shorts) the range support/resistance level where you entered [05:00]. However, speaker warns against "ultra conservative" stops that get hit by whipsaws [06:00].

### Take profit / exit
Target the opposite side of the range:
- For longs at range low: profit target at range high [04:30]
- For shorts at range high: profit target at range low [04:30]

### Invalidation / skip conditions
1. Range too narrow to trade profitably [02:00]
2. Price breaks out of defined range (invalidates range) [02:30]
3. Market is actually trending, not consolidating [00:30]

## Strategy 2: Fading Failed Breakouts

### Indicators and settings
- Range boundaries: upper and lower support/resistance [01:30]
- Failed breakout: price breaks above range high or below range low [08:30]

### Context / bias filter
Market is in consolidation phase with defined upper and lower range [01:30]. Breakout occurs but fails [08:30].

### Entry trigger
**Long entry (Fade failed breakdown):**
1. Price breaks below range low [08:30]
2. Wait for close back inside the range [08:30]
3. Or wait for a retest of the broken level [08:30]
4. Enter long on confirmation

**Short entry (Fade failed breakup):**
1. Price breaks above range high [08:30]
2. Wait for close back inside the range [08:30]
3. Or wait for a retest of the broken level [08:30]
4. Enter short on confirmation

### Stop loss
Placed below/above the failed breakout level [08:30]; specifically "the most recent lows (or highs)" from the breakout attempt [08:30].

### Take profit / exit
Target opposite side of range:
- For long fades of breakdowns: target range high [08:30]
- For short fades of breakups: target range low [08:30]

### Invalidation / skip conditions
If price continues through the range bound after breakout and closes outside range, the fade setup is invalidated and actual breakout has occurred [02:30].

## Strategy 3: Midrange Trading

### Indicators and settings
- Midrange level: 50% Fibonacci level between range high and range low [04:00]
- Direction rule: Above midrange price is bullish; below midrange is bearish [04:00], [08:30]

### Context / bias filter
Market in consolidation with defined range [04:00]. Identify all internal support/resistance levels for confluence with midrange level [04:00].

If range is wide enough, midrange can provide high probability setups [08:30].

**Trend alignment (optional):** "A trade that goes with the recent trend is more likely to play out compared to a trade against the trend" [08:30]. Prefer range setups aligned with previous trend [09:00].

### Entry trigger
**Long entry (Bullish from midrange):**
1. Price is above midrange level [04:00]
2. Price bounces from support

**Short entry (Bearish from midrange):**
1. Price is below midrange level [04:00]
2. Price bounces from resistance

### Stop loss
Conservative stop placement with consideration for whipsaw risk [05:00]. No specific level stated.

### Take profit / exit
Target at opposite range extremity or confluence level in direction of trade.

### Invalidation / skip conditions
Price breaking below midrange for longs or above midrange for shorts; trade reversal direction.

## General Range Trading Considerations

### Key concepts mentioned:
- Consolidation can be easy to spot after it forms but "real challenge to spot as it is forming" during transition from trend [00:30]
- Narrow range with "trouble areas and potential support and resistance levels grouped very close together" makes trading risky [02:00]
- Even minor support/resistance levels can cause price to hold in weak consolidating markets [03:30]
- Confluence (multiple levels aligning) increases probability of rejection/bounce [07:30]

### Stop loss and trade management philosophy:
Speaker emphasizes finding "Balance between Aggression and Conservation" [06:00]. 

**Risks of ultra-conservative stops:** "Managing the trade too tight can often result in a 'suffocated' trade as price whipsaws within the trading range" [06:00]. Moving stop to breakeven immediately "holds a very strong chance of hitting it" [06:30]. Results in breakeven trades or minor losses that don't benefit account equity [06:30].

**Alternative for ultra-conservative traders:** "Avoiding trading in a sideways market altogether might actually be a smarter move as that eliminates the risk entirely" [07:00].

### Optional indicators for confirmation:
- Relative Strength Index (RSI): identify overbought/oversold conditions at range extremes [09:00]
- Stochastics: identify overbought/oversold conditions [09:00]
- Divergence: powerful sign that range will hold; can be taken as trade confirmation [09:00]

### Notable claims and caveats
"Most markets don't trend all of the time" [09:30]; range trading is "very useful skill to have" [09:30]. Speaker notes difficulty in identifying range breakout vs. consolidation transition [00:30]. Acknowledges "unpredictable bounce" factor [05:30] and "seemingly unpredictable" price action [03:30].

No win rate claimed. No discussion of transaction costs, slippage, or commission. The framework is more about risk management and setup identification than specific entry/exit rules.
